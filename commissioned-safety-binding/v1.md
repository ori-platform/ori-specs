# Commissioned Safety Binding Contract v1

> Status: Design target, pre-ratification. No shipped consumer claims
> conformance. Amendable in place under the
> [pre-ratification exception](../VERSIONING.md#pre-ratification-exception).

A safety profile ([safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md))
activates from declared hardware. Declared hardware is not enough to fire a
cutoff. Sensor type presence tells a runtime that a current sensor exists; it
does not say which electrical circuit that sensor observes, which contactor
controls that circuit, whether the two are the same circuit, or what the load
does when the coil de-energises.

This contract defines the **commissioned safety binding**: an authenticated
record of the physical arrangement a safety profile evaluates against,
established at installation by whoever certified the electrical work, and
proven by measurement rather than asserted.

## Why This Contract Exists Separately

Three facts about a site cannot be derived from anything a backend knows.

**Association.** A site with two clamps and two contactors satisfies "a current
clamp plus a wired relay" twice over. Nothing in an inventory distinguishes the
correct pairing from the transposed one, and opening the wrong circuit is
itself a consequential physical action.

**Terminal state.** Whether isolating a load requires energising or
de-energising a coil is a property of the downstream wiring. Contact type does
not establish it — an NC contact is closed at rest, and what that means for the
load depends on where it sits in the circuit.

**Rated capacity.** A trip point is a capacity multiplied by a release-owned
constant. Whoever supplies the capacity moves the trip point. That makes it a
safety parameter regardless of the field it arrives in.

A schema alone answers none of these, which is why this contract requires a
physical proof and names a signer distinct from the provisioning authority.

## Normative Rule: Contact Type Is Not An Input

**Contact type MUST NOT appear as an input to any terminal-state
determination, in any implementation, at any layer.** Not as a default, not as
a fallback, not as a consistency check. An implementation that can name a
contact type and derive a coil state from it is non-conforming even when the
derivation happens to be correct for the site in front of it.

**Contact type is neither necessary nor sufficient** to establish the
protected-circuit outcome. It is not an input, so it cannot be a requirement
either: a contract that forbids deriving from contact type and then mandates a
contact type has made it an input through the back door. Which terminals a site
uses is an installation decision, constrained by the electrical design and the
relay's ratings, and the commissioning proof reports the outcome whatever was
chosen.

This supersedes the sentence in ori-specs#71 reading "Relays are wired normally
closed, so de-energising is already the safe state and a power cut produces it
without software." Both halves are wrong: the wiring claim, and the conclusion
drawn from it.

### De-energised Is Unavoidable, Not Universally Safe

Losing the controller de-energises the coil. That is physics, and no
implementation chooses it. It is the state at initialisation, at brownout, on a
crash, and on a power cut.

**Whether that state is safe is a per-zone question this contract does not
answer in advance.** A mapping may record `de_energised_terminal_state: closed`
— a load that reconnects when the coil drops — and such a zone is conforming.
Its safety case is then not made by software at all: it needs mechanical
latching, an independent interlock, or an operating procedure. Declaring
de-energised rest universally safe would erase exactly the sites that need that
answer, which is the failure this contract exists to prevent.

So: drive coils de-energised at initialisation and at brownout, because there
is nothing else to drive them to and a defined state beats an undefined one.
Do not describe the resulting circuit state as safe unless that zone's
commissioned mapping and its safety profile say it is.

`ori-edge-firmware` D-007 reached the "not sufficient" half independently. Its
title also says NC is "necessary", which its own body does not require — the
body asks only for a bench-verified terminal-state table and a commissioning
proof. That title wording should follow this rule.

## Scope And Trust Model

The trust root is the **commissioning-authority Ed25519 key**, held by the
party certifying the electrical installation.

| Key | Authority | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commissioning authority | Signs bindings: association, terminal state, calibration, rated capacity | Signs manifests, configs, commands, or evidence |
| Provisioning authority | Device identity, anchors, attested configuration | Signs a binding |
| Runtime command key | Inbound actuation commands | Signs a binding |

**The provisioning signer is excluded structurally, not by policy.** If the
provisioning backend could sign a binding, the commercial plane could make a
cutoff less sensitive by raising a declared capacity, without ever writing a
tier or a multiplier — the authority path ori-specs#71 exists to close,
entering through a field that looks like a measurement. A consumer MUST refuse
a binding whose signer is the provisioning authority for the same device, even
when the signature verifies.

This contract grants no tier authority. It does not author thresholds,
multipliers, or condition kinds, all of which remain release-owned.

### Key Selection, Generations, And Rotation

`signer_id` is a label. It is signed, so it cannot be altered in transit, and
it still proves nothing — a signer chooses its own label. **Authority is
decided by comparing key material.**

Every binding carries `signing_key`, the raw Ed25519 public key that signed it,
inside the signed body. A consumer configures two anchors out of band:

| Anchor | Source | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `commissioning_anchor_current` | `ORI_COMMISSIONING_ANCHOR_PUBLIC_KEY_B64` in the service environment the installer writes; delivered outside the binding | The generation that may author new bindings |
| `commissioning_anchor_previous` | `ORI_COMMISSIONING_ANCHOR_PREVIOUS_PUBLIC_KEY_B64`, same source, optional | The superseded generation, verify-only |

Both hold the canonical base64 of the raw 32-byte Ed25519 public key. The
installer writes them into the environment file the service unit loads — the
installation record — and no artifact of the configuration set names or
overrides them; the variable names are constants of this contract, for the
reason [runtime-config/v2](../runtime-config/v2.md) retires every anchor
selector. Absence of the current anchor with a binding present is
`unknown_signer` for that binding, because no key can be selected.

The provisioning anchor is already configured for
[runtime-config-signing/v1.md](../runtime-config-signing/v1.md) and is compared
against, not added.

Key **selection** happens before the signature. Every **authority verdict**
happens after it. The two are separate stages and the split is the whole point:

1. **`key_selection`.** If either commissioning anchor equals the provisioning
   anchor as raw bytes, refuse at configuration load with `anchor_collision` —
   the separation this contract rests on does not exist, and no document can
   repair it. Otherwise select exactly one candidate key: the anchor whose
   bytes equal `signing_key`. Matching no anchor is `unknown_signer`. **No
   verdict about authority is issued here, and no signature is attempted
   against a key the document did not name.**
2. **`signature`.** Verify against the single selected key. Failure is
   `bad_signature`.
3. **`authority`.** Only now, over a document proven to be signed by the key it
   names: the provisioning anchor is `wrong_authority`, and
   `commissioning_anchor_previous` is `superseded_signer`.

**An authority verdict must never be issued over an unverified document.** If
`wrong_authority` were decided at step 1, anyone could name the provisioning
public key — which is not a secret — over arbitrary bytes and manufacture a
`wrong_authority` event on any device they can reach. Deciding it after
verification means only the provisioning authority can cause its own verdict.

This is still not trial verification. The consumer verifies against exactly one
key, chosen by comparing key material, and never tries a second on failure.

**Rotation.** The previous generation is verify-only. The binding already in
force stays in force after rotation — it is not re-verified, and re-signing a
proven binding solely to change its key would demand a fresh commissioning
visit for a cryptographic event. New bindings must be signed by the current
generation, so a compromised key stops being able to author them the moment the
current anchor moves. This mirrors the runtime-gateway secret rotation posture:
previous verifies, current signs.

Issuance and custody of the commissioning key remain unsettled, and are recorded
under Open Questions rather than assumed.

## Document Shape

A binding document covers one `device_id` and carries one or more zones.

```json
{
  "binding": {
    "binding_seq": 3,
    "device_id": "energy-monitor-ikeja-01",
    "issued_at_ms": 1800000000000,
    "signer_id": "commissioning-lagos-ikeja-ade",
    "signing_key": "ed25519:<base64-32-byte-public-key>",
    "inventory_generation": 7,
    "supersedes": "sha256:<64-lowercase-hex>",
    "actor": "…",
    "reason": "…",
    "v": 1,
    "zones": [ … ]
  },
  "signature": "ed25519:<base64>"
}
```

| Field | Type | Required | Notes |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `v` | int | Yes | Must be `1`. |
| `binding_seq` | int | Yes | Strictly increasing per `device_id`. Distinct from every other counter in the platform. |
| `device_id` | string | Yes | Fleet alphabet. A binding signed for one device MUST NOT be accepted by another. |
| `issued_at_ms` | int | Yes | Audit only. Never a freshness proof — `binding_seq` is. |
| `signer_id` | string | Yes | Audit label only. Never an authority input. |
| `signing_key` | string | Yes | The raw Ed25519 public key that signed this document, `ed25519:<base64>`. Inside the signed body, so it cannot be altered without breaking the signature. |
| `inventory_generation` | integer | Yes | At least 1. The device/site inventory generation this binding was commissioned against. |
| `supersedes` | string \| null | Yes | Canonical hash of the prior binding, or `null` for the first. |
| `actor` | string | Yes | Who requested the change, per [firmware-mqtt-provisioning actor-and-reason](../firmware-mqtt-provisioning/v1.md#actor-and-reason). |
| `reason` | string | Yes | Why. Structurally required; absence is `malformed`. |
| `zones` | array | Yes | At least one. |

### Zone

```json
{
  "zone_id": "main-distribution",
  "rated_capacity": {
    "parameter": "rated_capacity_amps",
    "value": 10.0,
    "provenance": "nameplate"
  },
  "sensor": {
    "sensor_id": "load-current",
    "quantity": "current",
    "unit": "ampere",
    "range_min": 0.0,
    "range_max": 100.0,
    "direction": "positive_is_load_draw",
    "noise_floor": 0.05,
    "calibration_ref": "sct013-100-2026-08-19-a"
  },
  "actuator": { … },
  "proof": { … }
}
```

`rated_capacity.provenance` is one of `nameplate`, `installer_measured`,
`design_document`. It is recorded because a capacity is a safety parameter and
an audit needs to know what kind of claim it was.

`direction` is explicit and has no default. A clamp mounted backwards reports a
hazard as a fall in magnitude, and a profile evaluating an upper bound would
never fire.

### Actuator

The actuator record carries an identity and a **commissioned outcome
mapping**. Every mapping field is an independently commissioned fact. None is
derived from another.

```json
{
  "kind": "local_gpio",
  "identity": { "gpio_pin": 26, "active_high": false },
  "commissioned_mapping": {
    "open_protected_circuit": "de_energised",
    "close_protected_circuit": "energised",
    "de_energised_terminal_state": "open"
  }
}
```

| Field | Values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open_protected_circuit` | `energised` \| `de_energised` | The coil state that isolates the load |
| `close_protected_circuit` | `energised` \| `de_energised` | The coil state that restores the load |
| `de_energised_terminal_state` | `open` \| `closed` | What the load does when the coil loses power |

**These three are checked against each other, never inferred from each other.**
A consumer MUST refuse a mapping in which the two outcomes name the same coil
state, or in which `de_energised_terminal_state` disagrees with whichever
outcome maps to `de_energised`. Checking declared facts for contradiction is
not derivation: all three are separately established by the proof, and
disagreement means the proof did not establish what the document records.

`active_high` is an electrical property of the driver stage: it maps a GPIO
level to a coil state. It says nothing about the load, and it is not a
substitute for any mapping field. It is required for `kind: local_gpio` and has
no default.

#### Actuator kinds

| `kind` | `identity` fields | Backend |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `local_gpio` | `gpio_pin` (int), `active_high` (bool) | Runtime-local GPIO |
| `firmware_channel` | `firmware_device_id` (string), `channel` (string) | Signed command per [firmware-commands/v1.md](../firmware-commands/v1.md) |

`kind` is a closed vocabulary. An unrecognised kind is refused, never treated
as the nearest known one.

#### Firmware Command Semantics

[firmware-commands/v1.md](../firmware-commands/v1.md) carries the actions
`relay_open` and `relay_close`. **These are protected-circuit outcomes.** They
are the wire spelling of this contract's `open_protected_circuit` and
`close_protected_circuit`, exactly:

| Wire action | Semantic outcome |
| --- | --- |
| `relay_open` | `open_protected_circuit` |
| `relay_close` | `close_protected_circuit` |

They are **not** coil operations and **not** contact states. This is already
what `ori-edge-firmware` D-021 requires — the relay driver maps `relay_open`
through the board profile to a coil state, so `relay_open` cannot itself name a
coil state, and because the map is per channel it cannot name a contact state
either. The vocabulary was ambiguous in the documents and unambiguous in the
code; this table closes the documents.

The names are retained rather than renamed. `firmware-commands/v1` is an
implemented baseline with a wire vocabulary closed at the producer (D-015), and
renaming a shipped action to improve a document is a breaking change bought
with no safety gain. A future `v2` may align the spellings; until then the
mapping above is normative and an implementation MUST NOT interpret the wire
names any other way.

#### Delivery Lifecycle For `firmware_channel`

**One route, and it is not runtime provisioning.** The commissioned mapping
reaches a firmware device as part of the image or the sealed provisioning
record, before the device is capable of actuating. It MUST NOT be delivered to
a running device as ordinary configuration.

A mapping that arrives over the network after boot creates a window in which
the device has actuation capability and no proven mapping. D-021 forbids a
default for exactly that window, so the device would have to refuse all
actuation until the mapping landed — which is either a safety gap or a startup
dependency on the network, and it makes the mapping's authority depend on the
transport that carries it.

**A manifest reference is not sufficient, and must not be treated as proof.**
A `binding_ref` in the attested manifest proves only that the firmware *claims*
a relationship to a binding. A build or provisioning system can embed the
correct hash beside a wrong mapping, and every hash comparison a runtime
performs would then succeed while the device drives the opposite coil state —
the precise failure this contract exists to prevent, arriving through the
mechanism meant to detect it.

The device MUST therefore hold the mapping itself, in one of two forms:

1. **The exact signed binding**, verified on the device against the
   commissioning anchor by the same rules as any other consumer; or
2. **A commissioning-signed firmware profile** — a smaller object for devices
   that cannot carry or parse a full binding.

```json
{
  "firmware_profile": {
    "v": 1,
    "binding_hash": "sha256:<64-lowercase-hex>",
    "binding_seq": 3,
    "channel": "relay0",
    "commissioned_mapping": { … },
    "device_id": "energy-monitor-ikeja-01",
    "firmware_device_id": "ori-fw-7c9f2b3a",
    "signing_key": "ed25519:<base64-32-byte-public-key>"
  },
  "signature": "ed25519:<base64>"
}
```

The mapping is **inside the signed body**, bound to the binding it came from by
`binding_hash` and `binding_seq`, and scoped to one channel on one device. The
signer is the commissioning authority, checked by the same key-selection,
signature and authority sequence as a binding — so a build system cannot
commission a channel by signing its own profile.

Verification order for a profile is `parses`, `device_binding`,
`key_selection`, `signature`, `authority`, `binding_match`, `mapping_match`,
with `profile_binding_mismatch`, `profile_channel_mismatch` and
`profile_mapping_mismatch` as its additional verdicts.

**Key selection and authority are identical to a binding's, rotation
included.** A profile signed by the previous commissioning generation verifies
and is then refused as `superseded_signer`; it is not dismissed at
`key_selection` as an unknown key, which would report a rotated key of our own
as a stranger and lose the fact that the profile was once legitimately signed.
An `anchor_collision` involving *either* commissioning generation is fatal for
profiles exactly as for bindings. A runtime and a device
that both verify this object cannot disagree about which coil state opens the
circuit, because they are reading the same signed bytes.

The manifest keeps `binding_ref` for cheap detection and epoch reporting. It is
a hint, never an authority.

Recommissioning a firmware channel is therefore a provisioning event, not a
configuration edit. That is the correct cost: rewiring a contactor is a site
visit already.

### Proof

```json
{
  "method": "actuate_and_observe",
  "performed_at_ms": 1800000000000,
  "observations": [
    {
      "commanded": "open_protected_circuit",
      "coil_state": "de_energised",
      "gpio_level": "high",
      "load_present_before": true,
      "load_present_after": false,
      "sensor_before": 6.4,
      "sensor_after": 0.02,
      "terminal_state_observed": "open"
    },
    {
      "commanded": "close_protected_circuit",
      "coil_state": "energised",
      "gpio_level": "low",
      "load_present_before": false,
      "load_present_after": true,
      "sensor_before": 0.02,
      "sensor_after": 6.4,
      "terminal_state_observed": "closed"
    }
  ]
}
```

| `method` | Establishes | Proves the circuit leg |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `actuate_and_observe` | Association, direction, and both terminal states, by measurement on the live circuit | Yes |
| `pre_energisation` | Terminal states at the terminals with a meter, and sensor association against a controlled test load, before the site load is first energised | Yes |
| `undemonstrated` | Nothing. Records that no proof was possible and why. | No |

**None of these makes a zone eligible for autonomous actuation on its own.**
They establish one of two legs. The other is below, and a mapping is eligible
only when both hold.

#### A mapping has two proof legs, and one method proves one leg

A commissioned mapping asserts a chain: a control input reaches a driver, the
driver moves a coil, the coil changes a circuit, and a sensor observes the
change. No single measurement covers all of it, and the methods above cover
only the second half.

| Leg | What it establishes | Recorded in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `circuit` | The protected circuit, its terminal states, and that *this* sensor observes *that* circuit | `proof` |
| `control_path` | That the control input this binding names — `gpio_pin` at the declared `active_high` — is what moves *that* coil | `proof.control_path` |

**The circuit leg says nothing about the control path.** Every method above is
performed by whoever is at the panel, by whatever means is to hand: a meter, a
test switch, operating the contactor directly. That is legitimate evidence and
this contract does not require that software issued the command — but it leaves
open whether the runtime's own output reaches that coil at all, and whether it
reaches it in the polarity the binding claims.

`gpio_level` does not close it. It is checked for agreement with
`identity.active_high`, so it catches a self-inconsistent document and proves
nothing about which coil the pin drives. A binding can be internally perfect
and name the wrong pin.

**`active_high` is an assertion until the control leg proves it.** Getting it
wrong inverts every subsequent command: an instruction to release the coil
energises it. That is why an unproven control leg withholds actuation rather
than merely withholding a profile — see *Provisional and in force*.

```json
"control_path": {
  "method": "commanded_and_observed",
  "performed_at_ms": 1800000000000,
  "observations": [ … ]
}
```

| `control_path.method` | Establishes |
| --- | --- |
| `commanded_and_observed` | The runtime commanded a coil state through this binding's own `gpio_pin` at its declared `active_high`, and the load changed as the mapping predicts. **`local_gpio` only** |
| `undemonstrated` | Nothing. Records that no proof was possible and why. |

**`commanded_and_observed` is admissible only for `kind: local_gpio`, and a
`firmware_channel` zone carrying it is `malformed`.** The method names an
operation that exists for a pin and does not yet exist for a channel: the
firmware delivery lifecycle requires a device to hold its mapping before it can
actuate, so there is no designed way to command one through the path under
test. A contract that admitted the method for both would let a consumer bring a
firmware zone into force through a proof no design defines, which is worse than
having no method at all. Until that design lands, a `firmware_channel` zone's
control leg may only be absent or `undemonstrated`, and such a zone is
therefore always provisional.

`control_path` is **optional in the grammar and absent means unproven.** A
document written before this leg existed carries no claim about it, and reading
absence as proof would grant eligibility to every binding that predates the
rule. Absence denies; it never grants.

Its observations take the same shape as the circuit leg's and are checked by
the same rules, with one addition: for `kind: local_gpio`, `gpio_level` is
**required** on every observation and MUST agree with `identity.active_high`,
because the level actually driven is the evidence this leg exists to record.

A binding is signed only when its proof is complete for the method claimed. A
producer MUST NOT record a binding as proven that it did not demonstrate; an
`undemonstrated` zone carries a `reason` and no observations.

#### The proof is checked, not merely carried

A proof is not an attachment. **A consumer MUST verify that the recorded
observations agree with the mapping they claim to establish**, and refuse with
`proof_contradiction` when they do not.

**The instrument decides whether the load was present, not the reading.** Each
observation carries `load_present_before` and `load_present_after`: booleans
established by the commissioning instrument against the load itself — a clamp
meter, a continuity test, a lamp. They are required, and they are the
classifier. `sensor_before` and `sensor_after` are recorded evidence and are
never the decision input.

That split exists because "the sensor should read low when the circuit is open"
is not a specification. Low against what — a leakage allowance, a noise floor,
a percentage of nominal? Two conforming implementations would classify 0.02 A
differently while both following the prose, and the disagreement would surface
as one of them silently accepting a transposed binding.

For every observation:

- `coil_state` MUST equal the mapping's entry for `commanded`.
- `terminal_state_observed` MUST be `open` for `open_protected_circuit` and
  `closed` for `close_protected_circuit`.
- `load_present_before` and `load_present_after` MUST be `true, false` for
  `open_protected_circuit` and `false, true` for `close_protected_circuit`.
- Where both `sensor_before` and `sensor_after` are recorded, their difference
  MUST exceed the sensor's declared `noise_floor` and MUST move in the
  direction the load determination implies. A change the sensor cannot resolve
  is not a measurement, whatever the instrument reports.
- For `kind: local_gpio`, where `gpio_level` is present it MUST agree with
  `identity.active_high`: an energised coil is driven `high` when `active_high`
  is true and `low` when it is false. This is what makes a reversed driver
  stage detectable rather than asserted.
- Both outcomes MUST be observed. One-sided proof establishes one direction.

`noise_floor` is a required sensor field, in the sensor's own unit, and MUST be
greater than zero.

#### What this does and does not establish

Requiring `load_present_before` to be true before an open command is what stops
a proof on an idle circuit. An idle load reads low on both sides of an
actuation, so opening it demonstrates nothing at all — and a low reading
afterwards would otherwise look like success.

**A single observation pair does not conclusively exclude every transposition.**
Two circuits switched together by a shared upstream device, or two loads whose
duty cycles happen to coincide during the test, can both produce a
passing proof. What the rules above exclude is the large and common class:
a clamp on a different circuit that keeps drawing, a contactor that never
opened, a reversed driver stage, a change too small to measure, and a test run
against a dead load. Sites where a stronger guarantee is needed require
independent circuit isolation during the proof, which this contract does not
specify.

A proof whose observations contradict the mapping did not establish that
mapping, whoever signed it. A signature proves authorship, not correctness —
which is exactly why authorship alone is insufficient here.

#### Undemonstrated bindings

**A zone whose circuit leg is `undemonstrated` MUST NOT activate a profile
whose action actuates.** In staging and production this is a hardened startup
failure, consistent with the hardened-startup rule in
[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md). In
development posture the runtime starts with a consolidated WARNING and the
zone's actuating profiles inactive.

The zone still activates non-actuating profiles. Observation, alerting, and
Tier C proposal remain available on an unproven binding, because none of them
opens a circuit. Losing them as well would push installers toward recording a
proof they did not perform.

This is the resolution of the question ori-cli#34 defers to this contract. A
possibly-transposed autonomous cutoff is not safer than no autonomous cutoff:
it converts one hazard into two, and the second one arrives without warning on
a circuit nobody was watching.

### Provisional And In Force

Verification and authority are separate. A document that passes all twelve
stages has been shown to be authentic, self-consistent, and about this device.
It has not thereby been shown that its control path is what the mapping claims,
and only the second grants a consumer permission to move a coil.

| State | Reached by | The consumer may |
| --- | --- | --- |
| refused | failing any stage | nothing; the previous state is unchanged |
| **provisional** | passing every stage with any zone missing either leg | retain it, report it, and run the commissioning proof operation against it |
| **in force** | passing every stage with **both legs proven for every zone** | everything above, and connect and command the declared actuator |

**Both legs, per zone, and neither substitutes for the other.** A zone is in
force only when its `proof.method` is not `undemonstrated` *and* its
`proof.control_path.method` is `commanded_and_observed` on a `local_gpio`
actuator. One zone short of that leaves the whole binding provisional.

The two failures are independent and a proof of one says nothing about the
other. A proven control leg with an unproven circuit leg means the pin is known
to move the coil and nothing is known about which circuit that coil switches or
what its terminal states are — so commanding it opens an unidentified circuit,
which is the transposition hazard this contract was written for, reached by a
route that looks well proven. A proven circuit leg with an unproven control leg
is the converse and is what `#472` describes. Requiring only one of them
exchanges one gap for the other rather than closing either.

**A provisional binding is never in force.** A consumer MUST NOT connect the
actuator it names, MUST NOT command its coil — including the initialisation
command — and MUST NOT arm any profile that actuates it. It is retained,
auditable, and reported, and it is the only thing the proof operation below may
act on.

**Provisional is a separate record, not a weaker `accepted`.** It is not
entered into the freshness chain: `binding_seq` and `supersedes` continue to
describe the succession of documents that were *in force*, and a provisional
document that is never proven leaves that succession untouched. Reusing the
accepted lifecycle would make "accepted" mean two things one field apart, and
the first consumer to forget which it held would connect a relay under an
asserted polarity — which is the failure this state exists to prevent.

**Initialisation is not exempt.** Commanding a coil `de_energised` at startup is
a physical action derived from `active_high`; if that assertion is wrong the
command energises. A consumer holding only a provisional binding therefore
leaves the output untouched.

**An untouched output is not a safe circuit.** It preserves whatever this
zone's controller-loss state is, which the site must already have commissioned
and which this contract is explicit may be `closed`. A consumer MUST report
such a zone as **unavailable** — no protection claimed — and MUST NOT report it
as protected.

#### The Commissioning Proof Operation

The control leg cannot be proven without moving the coil once through the path
under test, and that command necessarily acts on a polarity that is still an
assertion. This is not made safe by the zone being unarmed. It is a **bounded
commissioning hazard**, accepted once, under supervision, to remove an
uncertainty that would otherwise sit under every later autonomous action.

A consumer offering it MUST constrain it to all of the following:

- **local only** — reachable solely from an authenticated local session on the
  device, never from a gateway topic, a fleet API, a remote command, or any
  configuration document, and never as a dispatcher action or an action tier;
- **provisional only** — it acts on a provisional binding's declared actuator
  and on nothing else, and is unavailable once that zone is in force;
- **consented per command** — a human present at the installation authorises
  each individual command. Both outcomes must be exercised, and each is its own
  authorisation: a single consent covering the pair would let the second
  command fire after the installer has seen the first do something unexpected,
  which is the moment consent exists to interrupt. The consent is recorded in
  the same audited event as the actuation it permitted;
- **single-use and short-lived** — one authorisation permits exactly one
  command and then expires, whether or not it was used;
- **carrying no authority** — it grants no tier, activates no profile, and its
  success is not an approval of anything else.

It records what was observed, not what was commanded. Both outcomes must be
commanded and both observed; a command whose effect was not observed proves
nothing and MUST NOT be recorded as a proof.

**Completion is a new document.** The installer signs a binding carrying both
legs, and that document — not the provisional record — is what may come into
force. Failure retires the provisional record and leaves the zone unprotected,
which is the honest outcome and not a degraded one.

**Commissioning precedes hardening, and the contract says so rather than
leaving it to be discovered.** Declared actuating hardware with no binding in
force refuses a hardened start, and a provisional binding is not in force — so
a device cannot be commissioned while already hardened. The proof operation
runs during installation, in development posture, and the deployment is
promoted once the binding is in force. That ordering is the real one: an
installer commissions a panel and then hands over a hardened device. A
consumer MUST NOT resolve the circularity by admitting a provisional binding
into a hardened start.

**`firmware_channel` zones need their own answer.** The delivery lifecycle
requires a firmware device to hold its mapping before it can actuate, so a
proof through the real authenticated command path cannot be arranged the way a
local pin can. That design is deliberately left open here rather than
approximated, and it does not hold up the local-GPIO leg.

## Bounds

Rated capacity is bounded by the declared hardware, so an implausible value
fails at the point a human can still see the wiring.

- `rated_capacity.value` MUST be greater than zero and MUST NOT exceed
  `sensor.range_max`. A capacity above the sensor's full scale describes a trip
  point the sensor cannot observe.
- Where a profile's condition multiplies capacity, the resulting trip point
  MUST also fall at or below `sensor.range_max`. A trip point beyond full scale
  is a cutoff that can never fire, and a saturating sensor reports it as a
  merely high reading.
- `range_min` MUST be less than `range_max`, and `value` MUST fall within the
  closed range.

These bounds do not replace the separate signer. A capacity within range is
still a safety parameter; the bound removes the absurd values, and the signer
separation removes the authority.

**The trip-point bound is not redundant with the capacity bound.** A 25 A
capacity on a 100 A clamp is unremarkable until a ×5 multiplier puts the trip
point at 125 A, past full scale, where a saturating sensor reports the hazard
as a merely high reading. The cutoff is then permanently unfireable while every
individual value looks reasonable. Authoring the reference vectors surfaced a
live instance of this: `ori.yaml.example` pairs a 10 A rated capacity with the
electrical overcurrent profile's ×5 multiplier, which on the 30 A clamp the
documentation recommends gives a 50 A trip point the sensor cannot reach.

## Disambiguation

Zones name hardware by identity, never by type.

- Two zones MUST NOT name the same actuator identity.
- Two zones MUST NOT name the same `sensor_id`.
- A `sensor_id` or actuator identity not present in the device's declared
  inventory is refused.
- A device with declared actuating hardware that no zone binds has no
  actuating profile for that hardware. This is a refusal in hardened posture,
  not a silent deactivation.

Ambiguity is refused rather than resolved. There is no nearest-match rule, no
ordering preference, and no single-candidate shortcut — a site with exactly one
clamp and one contactor still requires an explicit binding, because the next
revision of that site will have two.

## Change Lifecycle

Every change is a new binding document with `binding_seq` incremented and
`supersedes` naming the canonical hash of the one it replaces.

- A consumer MUST reject a binding whose `binding_seq` does not strictly exceed
  the accepted one, and whose `supersedes` does not match the accepted
  document's hash.
- The prior document is retained. A consumer MUST be able to produce the
  binding in force at any past time for audit.
- **Replacement, recalibration, rewiring, and polarity change all invalidate
  the proof.** A new actuator does not inherit the mapping the previous one was
  proven to have. A revision changing any actuator identity field, any mapping
  field, or `calibration_ref` MUST carry a fresh proof for that zone or record
  it as `undemonstrated`.
- A revision that changes only `rated_capacity` retains the proof but is
  re-bounds-checked.

## Delivery And Verification

A binding reaches the runtime as a signed document alongside the config, and
reaches firmware — for `firmware_channel` zones — as the source of the board
profile the device is built or provisioned with.

For the runtime the document is the envelope form above, UTF-8 JSON, at
`commissioning/binding.json` under the installation's data directory — the
directory that holds `ori.yaml` and the state store. The path is a constant of
this contract and of the installer, never a configuration value: a document
that could name where the next binding is read from would choose its own
successor. The runtime reads the file at startup; a file that fails any stage
leaves the previously accepted binding in force, exactly as a document
arriving by any other route would. Retained state lives in the state store,
not in the file.

### Verification Order

There is exactly one order, and it is the `acceptance_order` recorded in the
golden corpus. Fail closed at each stage:

| # | Stage | Refuses with |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | `parses` | `malformed` |
| 2 | `device_id` | `wrong_device` |
| 3 | `key_selection` | `anchor_collision`, `unknown_signer` |
| 4 | `signature` | `bad_signature` |
| 5 | `authority` | `wrong_authority`, `superseded_signer` |
| 6 | `freshness` | `stale` |
| 7 | `mapping_self_consistency` | `mapping_contradiction` |
| 8 | `proof_consistency` | `proof_contradiction`, `stale_proof` |
| 9 | `bounds` | `out_of_bounds` |
| 10 | `disambiguation` | `ambiguous_binding` |
| 11 | `inventory` | `unknown_hardware`, `unbound_actuator` |
| 12 | `activation_posture` | `undemonstrated_binding` |

`anchor_collision` is evaluated at configuration load, before any binding is
seen; it is listed at `key_selection` because that is the stage whose
precondition it is.

A binding failing any step is not partially applied. The previously accepted
binding remains in force unchanged.

**Order is normative, not incidental.** A refusal is only evidence that a
consumer performed a check if every earlier check passed, which is why every
reject vector carries a `stage` field naming where its verdict is decided, and
a conforming verifier must be refused by that stage and no earlier one.

### The Inventory Generation

A binding describes an arrangement of specific hardware. The hardware a device
declares can change — a clamp is moved, a contactor is replaced, a second
circuit is added — and a binding commissioned before that change describes a
site that no longer exists.

`inventory_generation` names the device/site inventory generation the binding
was commissioned against. It is an **integer of at least one, scoped to
`device_id`**, allocated by the provisioning-signed inventory authority, and it
sits **inside the signed body**: a value an intermediary could edit would let a
stale binding claim currency.

**Zero is refused.** A binding is commissioned against an inventory, so there
has to be one for it to name; a document claiming to describe hardware that was
never declared is a binding to absence, not an early state. Zero could become
admissible only if the device/site contract defined it as a real signed
inventory generation, and no such contract does. Until one says otherwise, the
floor is one.

**The number only identifies an inventory if the authority that allocates it
guarantees two things**, and the device/site contract must state both:

- generations are **monotonic** per device, and
- a generation is **never reused for different inventory contents**.

Without the second rule a binding can name generation 7 truthfully while the
device holds a different generation 7, and every check downstream passes over a
mismatch it cannot see. This contract depends on that guarantee and does not
provide it.

#### What this contract does and does not decide

It defines the field, its type, its scope and its signing. **It does not define
what a consumer does when the generations differ.** That is a cross-artifact
state transition between a device/site document and a binding, and it belongs
to the orchestration contract, along with the invariant those two artifacts
share:

```text
active device/site inventory generation
    == active commissioned binding inventory_generation
```

**No intermediate state grants actuation authority.** A consumer holding a
device/site generation and a binding that names a different one does not have a
usable configuration set, and it must not resolve that by keeping part of one:
a new inventory beside an old mapping is a partially applied set, and the old
mapping describes hardware the device no longer claims to have. The orchestrator
either retains the previous mutually consistent pair whole, or enters an
explicit degraded posture — it does not compose a working device out of two
incompatible halves.

### Retained State

A consumer retains, from the binding in force, at least: `binding_seq`, the
canonical hash, and per zone the actuator identity, the commissioned mapping,
the calibration reference, and the time each proof leg was performed. Retention
is already required for audit; the revision rule reads the same record, so a
changed actuator cannot inherit the proof of the one it replaced.

A provisional binding is retained **separately** and never as the binding in
force, with the observations the proof operation gathered against it. Keeping
one record for both states is what would let a consumer read a provisional
document as authority, so the separation is structural rather than a flag.

### Canonical Bytes

Signatures are over the canonical serialisation of the `binding` object using
the canonical form of
[gateway-mqtt-canonical-json/v1.md](../gateway-mqtt-canonical-json/v1.md):
recursively sorted keys, no insignificant whitespace, RFC 8259 escaping,
caller-ordered arrays.

Numbers are additionally constrained to the D-011 agreement zone as required by
[evidence/v2.md](../evidence/v2.md) — integers within ±(2^53 − 1), non-integers
finite and either exactly zero or of magnitude in `[1e-4, 1e16)`, fixed
notation with a mandatory fractional part. The stricter zone applies here and
not on gateway telemetry because a C consumer participates in this contract:
firmware verifying its own board profile must produce the same bytes as a Go
producer and a Python runtime.

#### Spelling Is Grammar, And The Schema Decides It

**A number's spelling is fixed by the declared type of the field it sits in,
not by its value, and a verifier reading bytes MUST refuse any other spelling
as `malformed` at `parses`.**

A field the [field-type table](#field-types) declares `integer` is spelled with
no fractional part and no exponent. A field it declares `number` is spelled in
fixed notation **with** a fractional part — `10.0`, never `10` — and in the
shortest form that round-trips to the same value: `1E1`, `10.00` and `10` are
each refused for a `number` field.

The rule is over the type rather than the value because JSON does not
distinguish them and only the schema knows which was meant. D-011 phrases its
zone over values, and read that way `10.0` is an integer whose canonical
spelling would be `10`; a producer building a document from typed values
spells it `10.0` because the field is a capacity, not a count. Two producers
reading the same sentence differently is the divergence this contract exists to
remove, so the sentence is made to depend on something both can see.

**A verifier that re-canonicalises before comparing has not applied this rule.**
Decoding `1E1` to a value and re-emitting `10.0` reproduces the signed preimage,
so the signature verifies and the document is accepted — while a consumer
comparing bytes refuses the same file. Two conforming-looking implementations
then disagree about a document neither considers wrong, which is exactly the
argument this contract already makes for base64: strict decoding admits four
spellings of one key, and only decode-then-re-encode equality admits one. The
same reasoning gives the same rule here, and a C consumer verifying its own
board profile byte for byte cannot implement any other one.

Because a decoded document no longer carries the spelling it arrived with, this
rule is exercised by `raw_reject_cases` rather than by the ordinary sections.

### The Closed Grammar

`parses` is a **closed** grammar, and closed at every level rather than only
the top. Every object admits exactly its declared keys: an unrecognised key is
`malformed`, never ignored. A consumer that silently drops a field it does not
read turns a stale or misspelt safety declaration into one that appears, to
whoever wrote it, to have taken effect — and `contact_type` is the field this
contract most needs to be unrepresentable rather than unread.

| Object | Exactly these keys |
| --- | --- |
| binding | `v`, `binding_seq`, `device_id`, `issued_at_ms`, `signer_id`, `signing_key`, `inventory_generation`, `supersedes`, `actor`, `reason`, `zones` |
| zone | `zone_id`, `rated_capacity`, `sensor`, `actuator`, `proof` |
| `control_path` | `method`, `performed_at_ms`, `observations`, and `reason` for `undemonstrated` |
| `rated_capacity` | `parameter`, `value`, `provenance` |
| `sensor` | `sensor_id`, `quantity`, `unit`, `range_min`, `range_max`, `direction`, `noise_floor`, `calibration_ref` |
| `actuator` | `kind`, `identity`, `commissioned_mapping` |
| `commissioned_mapping` | the three outcome keys |
| firmware profile | `v`, `binding_hash`, `binding_seq`, `channel`, `commissioned_mapping`, `device_id`, `firmware_device_id`, `signing_key` |
| binding envelope | `binding`, `signature` |
| profile envelope | `firmware_profile`, `signature` |

**Both envelope forms carry the same rule, and both are exercised.** A corpus
covering only the binding envelope cannot tell a conforming implementation from
one that validates bindings strictly and profiles loosely — and the profile
envelope is the shape a constrained C consumer receives, so it is the worse of
the two to leave unexercised.

**The envelope is closed too.** A grammar that closes the signed object and
leaves its container open is not closed. A field beside the signature sits
*outside* the signed bytes and is therefore unauthenticated by construction, so
ignoring it lets an intermediary attach content a careless consumer might read;
and a signature of the wrong length would otherwise reach a verifier that may
raise on it rather than refuse it.

**An object MUST NOT carry the same key twice, at any level, and a verifier
reading bytes MUST refuse one during parsing.** The rule is inherited from
[evidence/v2.md](../evidence/v2.md) and is named here because it is invisible
to every check that follows it: by the time a grammar sees a decoded object the
duplicate has already collapsed, and which occurrence survived is a property of
the parser rather than of the document. A last-wins parser verifies the
signature over the value it kept while a first-wins parser reads the other from
the same file, so the canonical form of such an object is undefined and the
bytes are refused rather than resolved. This holds even when both occurrences
carry the same value: a rule that admitted the harmless case would have to
decode both before it could tell them apart.

#### Rules Only Bytes Can Carry

Three rules in this section — number spelling, duplicate keys, and unpaired
surrogates — cannot be expressed by a decoded document, because decoding is
what destroys the evidence for them. They are exercised by
**`raw_reject_cases`**, whose entries carry the wire bytes of a whole envelope
as `envelope_hex` instead of a decoded object, alongside the same
`verifier_context`, `reason` and `stage` every other reject case carries.

A consumer MUST exercise that section through the entry point that takes bytes,
which is the one a device actually uses. Feeding it through a decoded-object
entry point tests nothing: the decode is the step under test.

**`identity` is closed per kind**, not per contract: `local_gpio` admits exactly
`gpio_pin` and `active_high`; `firmware_channel` admits exactly
`firmware_device_id` and `channel`. A GPIO identity on a firmware channel names
an actuator that does not exist, and would leave `active_high` unread on a
device that has no pin.

**`proof` is closed per method.** `actuate_and_observe` and `pre_energisation`
admit `method`, `performed_at_ms` and a non-empty `observations`;
`undemonstrated` admits those plus `reason`, and its `observations` MUST be
empty — an unproven zone carrying observations is claiming a proof it also says
it does not have. Each additionally admits an optional `control_path`.

**`control_path` is closed the same way**, over its own method vocabulary:
`commanded_and_observed` admits `method`, `performed_at_ms` and a non-empty
`observations`, and only on a `local_gpio` actuator; `undemonstrated` admits those plus `reason` with empty
`observations`. Absence of the whole object is unproven, and carrying it with
`method: undemonstrated` says the same thing with a reason attached.

**Observations** require `commanded`, `coil_state`, `load_present_before`,
`load_present_after` and `terminal_state_observed`, and admit only
`gpio_level`, `sensor_before`, `sensor_after` and `instrument` besides. In a
**`control_path`** observation on a `local_gpio` actuator `gpio_level` is
**required** rather than optional: the level actually driven is the evidence
that leg exists to record, and a control-path proof without it has recorded the
command and not the control path.
`gpio_level` is admissible only for `local_gpio` — a pin level is meaningless
for a channel with no pin, so its presence is a shape error rather than an
ignorable extra. `sensor_before` and `sensor_after` are paired: one without the
other cannot show a change, and admitting it would let a proof skip the
noise-floor rule by omitting a field.

**Closed value vocabularies**: `kind`, `provenance` (`nameplate`,
`installer_measured`, `design_document`), `method`, `gpio_level` (`high`,
`low`), the coil and circuit states, and `commanded`.

**A vocabulary value MUST be checked as a string before it is tested for
membership.** This is not pedantry about ordering: in several languages a
membership test against an array or object raises rather than returning false
— Python's `in` against a `set` raises `TypeError` on an unhashable value — so
a verifier that tests membership first crashes on `"kind": []` instead of
refusing it. Every vocabulary in this contract has that shape.

**Strings MUST be valid UTF-8, with no unpaired surrogates.** A JSON document
carries `\ud800` as six ASCII characters and is itself perfectly valid UTF-8;
the surrogate appears only after decoding, where it survives every type and
vocabulary check and reaches canonicalisation.

**What happens there differs by language, which is the whole problem.**
Measured, not assumed:

| Implementation | Decoding `"\ud800"` then serialising |
| --- | --- |
| CPython `json`, `ensure_ascii=False` | raises `UnicodeEncodeError` |
| Go `encoding/json` (1.26) | **no error**; substitutes U+FFFD and emits `ef bf bd` |

A Python verifier crashes and a Go verifier succeeds with different bytes.
Neither reports a shared reason, and the two disagree about the signature over
a document neither considers malformed — the exact divergence class the
canonical-byte rules exist to remove, arriving through a value that every
other rule in this contract accepts. So it is refused at `parses`, before
canonicalisation and before any signature is checked.

This rule is exercised by `raw_reject_cases`, the corpus section that carries
wire bytes rather than decoded objects. It cannot be expressed anywhere else:
the other sections store **decoded** documents that are expected to be
canonically serialisable, and an unpaired surrogate cannot satisfy that model.

**Encodings are part of the grammar, not of verification.**

`signing_key` is `ed25519:` followed by the **canonical** RFC 4648 encoding of
exactly 32 bytes. `signature`, in either envelope, is `ed25519:` followed by the
canonical encoding of exactly 64 bytes.

Canonical means the standard alphabet, mandatory padding, and **decode-then-
re-encode equality**. Strict decoding alone is not sufficient and must not be
specified as though it were: a validating decoder rejects characters outside the
alphabet and says nothing about the unused low bits of the final character. Four
distinct strings decode to the same 32-byte key and all four pass strict
validation — verified against CPython's `base64.b64decode(..., validate=True)`,
which accepts `…l3hzc=`, `…l3hzd=`, `…l3hze=` and `…l3hzf=` for one key. Each is
a different signing input naming the same key, and two implementations would
disagree about a document neither considers malformed. Round-trip equality is
the only decoder property that admits exactly one spelling, and it fixes
alphabet, padding and pad bits together.

`binding_hash` in a firmware profile, like `supersedes` in a binding, is
`sha256:` followed by 64 lowercase hex; `supersedes` additionally admits `null`.
Without that shape the tie between a profile and the binding it came from is
unverifiable. `binding_seq` and `inventory_generation` are each an integer of
at least **one**, and integer is meant literally: `7.0` is not `7` here. They
are different canonical bytes and therefore different signing inputs, so a
verifier deciding the type by numeric value rather than by spelling admits a
form this contract does not define. Two implementations will not agree by
default. Python's `7.0 == 7` is true and `isinstance(7.0, int)` is false; Go's
`encoding/json` decodes both to `float64` when the target is `interface{}` —
the shape a schema-driven verifier uses — while rejecting `7.0` outright when
the target is a typed `int` field. Both were measured, and neither is a
substitute for checking the decoded type explicitly.

A verifier MUST return `malformed` for each of these rather than raising: a
verifier that throws on malformed input has not refused it, and a caller cannot
distinguish that from a crash. That obligation is absolute and covers input
chosen to break a decoder or a lookup rather than a check — arrays and objects
where a vocabulary is expected, wrong types where an object is expected, and
strings that cannot be encoded.

#### Field Types

Key sets alone are not a grammar. Two implementations agreeing on which keys
exist can still disagree on what may sit behind them, so every field's type and
constraint is normative here rather than left to be inferred from the examples.

| Field | Type | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `v` | integer | exactly `1` |
| `binding_seq`, `firmware_profile.binding_seq` | integer | at least 1 |
| `inventory_generation` | integer | at least 1; not a float, not a boolean |
| `issued_at_ms`, `performed_at_ms` | integer | not a float, not a string |
| `device_id`, `signer_id`, `actor`, `reason`, `zone_id` | string | non-empty after trimming whitespace |
| `firmware_device_id`, `channel` | string | non-empty |
| `sensor_id`, `quantity`, `unit`, `direction`, `calibration_ref` | string | non-empty |
| `rated_capacity.parameter` | string | non-empty |
| `rated_capacity.value` | number | — |
| `range_min`, `range_max` | number | `range_min` < `range_max` |
| `noise_floor` | number | greater than zero |
| `sensor_before`, `sensor_after` | number | present together or not at all |
| `identity.gpio_pin` | integer | — |
| `identity.active_high` | boolean | — |
| `load_present_before`, `load_present_after` | boolean | — |
| `observation.instrument` | string | non-empty |
| `zones` | array of object | non-empty |
| `observations` | array of object | empty for `undemonstrated`, non-empty otherwise |
| `signing_key` | string | `ed25519:` + canonical base64 of 32 bytes |
| `signature` | string | `ed25519:` + canonical base64 of 64 bytes |
| `binding_hash` | string | `sha256:` + 64 lowercase hex |
| `supersedes` | string or null | `sha256:` + 64 lowercase hex when present |

**A boolean is not a number and a number is not a boolean.** JSON distinguishes
them and some languages do not — in Python `True` is an instance of `int`, so a
naive numeric check accepts `"noise_floor": true`. An implementation MUST
exclude booleans where a number is required, and MUST NOT accept `0` or `1`
where a boolean is required.

Empty and whitespace-only strings are `malformed` wherever a string is
required. A field that carries no information is not a field that was supplied,
and `signer_id: ""` in an audit record is worse than its absence because it
looks answered.

### Error Semantics

| Verdict | Decided at (stage name as recorded in the corpus) | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `malformed` | parses | Shape, grammar, unknown `kind`, unknown key, missing required field, number outside the agreement zone, number spelled other than its field's declared type requires, duplicate object key, unpaired surrogate |
| `wrong_device` | device_id (binding), device_binding (profile) | Document is addressed to a different device |
| `anchor_collision` | key_selection | A commissioning anchor and the provisioning anchor are the same key material; evaluated at configuration load |
| `wrong_authority` | authority | The verified signer is the provisioning anchor |
| `superseded_signer` | authority | The verified signer is the previous commissioning generation |
| `unknown_signer` | key_selection | `signing_key` matches no configured anchor |
| `bad_signature` | signature | Signature does not verify against `signing_key` |
| `stale` | freshness | `binding_seq` not strictly increasing, or `supersedes` mismatch |
| `mapping_contradiction` | mapping_self_consistency | Mapping fields disagree with one another |
| `proof_contradiction` | proof_consistency | Observations disagree with the mapping they claim to establish |
| `stale_proof` | proof_consistency | A revision changed actuator identity, mapping, or calibration without a fresh proof |
| `out_of_bounds` | bounds | Capacity, trip point, or range violation |
| `ambiguous_binding` | disambiguation | Duplicate sensor or actuator identity across zones |
| `unknown_hardware` | inventory | Named sensor or actuator absent from declared inventory |
| `unbound_actuator` | inventory | Declared actuating hardware that no zone binds |

## Consumer Obligations

- A consumer MUST NOT actuate through an actuator whose zone has no accepted
  binding, and MUST NOT fall back to any default mapping when one is absent.
  Absence of a mapping is a refusal to actuate, never an assumption.
- A consumer MUST re-apply the mapping lookup at each actuation, not cache a
  resolved coil state across a binding revision.
- A consumer MUST record which `binding_seq` was in force for every actuation
  it logs, so an action can later be attributed to the physical arrangement it
  was taken under.
- Attestation quality never gates a trip. Where a binding is accepted and a
  reading is credible, the profile fires and any evidence gap is recorded.

The generator and the first verifier are both Python, which satisfies
reproduction but not independence: they were written from this text rather than
from each other, and that is weaker than a second language. The Go producer in
`ori-cli` is the natural second implementation, and ratification waits for it.

### Golden Vectors

The authoritative corpus is
[binding-vectors-v1.json](binding-vectors-v1.json), held **here**, beside this
document. Its SHA-256 is
`c1d6f25aaa399897065bccf0041743edaebfcecf52d660cb70940cf88e5b980a`.

It carries 4 accept and 47 reject binding cases — at least one per verdict
above, plus the transposition, idle-load, noise-floor, polarity, trip-point,
rotation, inventory-generation and anchor-collision cases that motivate the
rules — and 1 accept and 12 reject firmware-profile cases, and 11 envelope
reject cases across both envelope forms. The reference site is two clamps and
two contactors whose zones carry **opposite** commissioned mappings, so an
implementation that derives one outcome from a convention rather than reading
it fails exactly one zone.

Implementing repositories copy the file byte-for-byte and pin that digest,
never generating its expectations from their own behaviour. The artifact lives
here rather than only its digest, for the reasons given in
[firmware-commands/v1.md](../firmware-commands/v1.md#golden-vectors): a CODEOWNER
asked to ratify a bare hash can only confirm that it is a hexadecimal number,
and two constants coordinated by hand are not a parity mechanism.

Every case records `canonical_hex`, `canonical_sha256` and `message_hex`, so a
producer's bytes are checkable without running a verifier, and a verifier's
verdicts are checkable without running a producer.

Reject cases carry two fields that make them meaningful. **Both are required
on every reject case in every corpus** — binding, firmware profile, and
envelope. A firmware profile is verified by its
own ordered stages, so its rejections name them as a binding's do, and its
signature stage is exercised by its own vector rather than borrowed from the
binding corpus. Envelope rejections are all decided at `parses`, since the
wrapper is grammar.

- `reason` is the verdict and `stage` names where it must be decided, on every
  case rather than only in the top-level `acceptance_order` or
  `firmware_profile_acceptance_order`. A case that refuses for the right reason
  at the wrong stage is not evidence that the named check exists, and a
  verifier must assert both.
- `signature_valid` distinguishes a refusal decided before the signature check
  from one decided after it. A binding, freshness or bounds refusal only
  demonstrates that check ran if the signature was good.

**Ratification requires both halves.** The corpus is not ratified until a
generator reproduces the file byte-for-byte and an **independent verifier, in a
different language from the generator, and not sharing its code**, accepts
every accept case and refuses every reject case at its declared stage. A corpus
checked only by the tool that wrote it certifies whatever that tool does,
including its defects.

Both artifacts exist for this corpus and are the reason several defects were
caught before ratification, including a reference site whose own trip point sat
beyond its sensor's full scale and a verifier classifying load presence from a
hard-coded current threshold. They are named under Required Implementation
Artifacts and land with the first implementing repository; `ori-specs` is
documentation-only and holds neither.

## Open Questions

- **Commissioning key custody.** Issuance, rotation, and revocation of the
  commissioning key are not settled here. ori-cli#34 explicitly excludes it.
  Until settled, a deployment has no defined recovery from a lost or
  compromised commissioning key, and this contract does not pretend otherwise.
- **Multi-device zones.** A zone whose sensor is on the runtime host and whose
  actuator is a firmware channel is expressible above, but the failure
  semantics when the firmware device is unreachable at binding-verification
  time are not specified.
- **Re-proof cadence.** Whether a proof expires — and what a site does about a
  binding proven three years ago — is unspecified. Wiring changes without a
  document change are outside what any signature can detect.
- **Independence of the second implementation.** Both current vector tools are
  Python. Until a verifier exists in another language, the corpus demonstrates
  internal consistency rather than cross-language agreement, which is the
  property the canonical-bytes rules exist for.
