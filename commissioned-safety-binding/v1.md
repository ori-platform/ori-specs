# Commissioned Safety Binding Contract v1

> Status: Design target, pre-ratification. No shipped consumer claims
> conformance. Amendable in place under the
> [pre-ratification exception](../VERSIONING.md#pre-ratification-exception).

A safety profile ([safety-profile, ori-specs#71](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-specs/issues/71))
activates from declared hardware. Declared hardware is not enough to fire a
cutoff. Sensor type presence tells a runtime that a current sensor exists; it
does not say which electrical circuit that sensor observes, which contactor
controls that circuit, whether the two are the same circuit, or what the load
does when the coil de-energises.

This contract defines the **commissioned safety binding**: an authenticated
record of the physical arrangement a safety profile evaluates against,
established at installation by whoever certified the electrical work, and
proven by measurement rather than asserted.

## Why This Contract Exists Separately

Three facts about a site cannot be derived from anything a backend knows.

**Association.** A site with two clamps and two contactors satisfies "a current
clamp plus a wired relay" twice over. Nothing in an inventory distinguishes the
correct pairing from the transposed one, and opening the wrong circuit is
itself a consequential physical action.

**Terminal state.** Whether isolating a load requires energising or
de-energising a coil is a property of the downstream wiring. Contact type does
not establish it — an NC contact is closed at rest, and what that means for the
load depends on where it sits in the circuit.

**Rated capacity.** A trip point is a capacity multiplied by a release-owned
constant. Whoever supplies the capacity moves the trip point. That makes it a
safety parameter regardless of the field it arrives in.

A schema alone answers none of these, which is why this contract requires a
physical proof and names a signer distinct from the provisioning authority.

## Normative Rule: Contact Type Is Not An Input

**Contact type MUST NOT appear as an input to any terminal-state
determination, in any implementation, at any layer.** Not as a default, not as
a fallback, not as a consistency check. An implementation that can name a
contact type and derive a coil state from it is non-conforming even when the
derivation happens to be correct for the site in front of it.

This supersedes the sentence in ori-specs#71 reading "Relays are wired normally
closed, so de-energising is already the safe state and a power cut produces it
without software." Normally-closed wiring is necessary and not sufficient — the
same conclusion `ori-edge-firmware` reached independently in D-007. De-energised
rest is the safe state **because a commissioning proof showed that it is**, per
zone, and for no other reason.

The corollary is that a de-energised default is still the right rest state to
drive at initialisation and at brownout. What changes is the justification: it
is the commissioned fact, not the relay vocabulary.

## Scope And Trust Model

The trust root is the **commissioning-authority Ed25519 key**, held by the
party certifying the electrical installation.

| Key | Authority | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commissioning authority | Signs bindings: association, terminal state, calibration, rated capacity | Signs manifests, configs, commands, or evidence |
| Provisioning authority | Device identity, anchors, attested configuration | Signs a binding |
| Runtime command key | Inbound actuation commands | Signs a binding |

**The provisioning signer is excluded structurally, not by policy.** If the
provisioning backend could sign a binding, the commercial plane could make a
cutoff less sensitive by raising a declared capacity, without ever writing a
tier or a multiplier — the authority path ori-specs#71 exists to close,
entering through a field that looks like a measurement. A consumer MUST refuse
a binding whose signer is the provisioning authority for the same device, even
when the signature verifies.

This contract grants no tier authority. It does not author thresholds,
multipliers, or condition kinds, all of which remain release-owned.

### Key Selection, Generations, And Rotation

`signer_id` is a label. It is signed, so it cannot be altered in transit, and
it still proves nothing — a signer chooses its own label. **Authority is
decided by comparing key material.**

Every binding carries `signing_key`, the raw Ed25519 public key that signed it,
inside the signed body. A consumer configures two anchors out of band:

| Anchor | Source | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `commissioning_anchor_current` | Installation record, delivered outside the binding | The generation that may author new bindings |
| `commissioning_anchor_previous` | Same, optional | The superseded generation, verify-only |

The provisioning anchor is already configured for
[runtime-config-signing/v1.md](../runtime-config-signing/v1.md) and is compared
against, not added.

Authority is evaluated **before** the signature, by comparison alone:

1. If either commissioning anchor equals the provisioning anchor as raw bytes,
   the consumer refuses at configuration load with `anchor_collision`. The
   separation this contract rests on does not exist, and no document can repair
   it. This check runs before any binding is seen.
2. `signing_key` equal to the provisioning anchor is `wrong_authority`.
3. `signing_key` equal to `commissioning_anchor_previous` is
   `superseded_signer`.
4. `signing_key` equal to neither anchor is `unknown_signer`.
5. Otherwise the signature is verified against `signing_key`.

**This is why `wrong_authority` is reachable without trial verification.** A
consumer never attempts a signature against the provisioning key to discover
which authority signed a document. It reads the key the document names, compares
it to configured key material, and only then verifies — and a document naming a
key it does not hold fails at step 5 with `bad_signature`, so a lying
`signing_key` buys nothing.

**Rotation.** The previous generation is verify-only. The binding already in
force stays in force after rotation — it is not re-verified, and re-signing a
proven binding solely to change its key would demand a fresh commissioning
visit for a cryptographic event. New bindings must be signed by the current
generation, so a compromised key stops being able to author them the moment the
current anchor moves. This mirrors the runtime-gateway secret rotation posture:
previous verifies, current signs.

Issuance and custody of the commissioning key remain unsettled, and are recorded
under Open Questions rather than assumed.

## Document Shape

A binding document covers one `device_id` and carries one or more zones.

```json
{
  "binding": {
    "binding_seq": 3,
    "device_id": "energy-monitor-ikeja-01",
    "issued_at_ms": 1800000000000,
    "signer_id": "commissioning-lagos-ikeja-ade",
    "signing_key": "ed25519:<base64-32-byte-public-key>",
    "supersedes": "sha256:<64-lowercase-hex>",
    "actor": "…",
    "reason": "…",
    "v": 1,
    "zones": [ … ]
  },
  "signature": "ed25519:<base64>"
}
```

| Field | Type | Required | Notes |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `v` | int | Yes | Must be `1`. |
| `binding_seq` | int | Yes | Strictly increasing per `device_id`. Distinct from every other counter in the platform. |
| `device_id` | string | Yes | Fleet alphabet. A binding signed for one device MUST NOT be accepted by another. |
| `issued_at_ms` | int | Yes | Audit only. Never a freshness proof — `binding_seq` is. |
| `signer_id` | string | Yes | Audit label only. Never an authority input. |
| `signing_key` | string | Yes | The raw Ed25519 public key that signed this document, `ed25519:<base64>`. Inside the signed body, so it cannot be altered without breaking the signature. |
| `supersedes` | string \| null | Yes | Canonical hash of the prior binding, or `null` for the first. |
| `actor` | string | Yes | Who requested the change, per [firmware-mqtt-provisioning actor-and-reason](../firmware-mqtt-provisioning/v1.md#actor-and-reason). |
| `reason` | string | Yes | Why. Structurally required; absence is `malformed`. |
| `zones` | array | Yes | At least one. |

### Zone

```json
{
  "zone_id": "main-distribution",
  "rated_capacity": {
    "parameter": "rated_capacity_amps",
    "value": 10.0,
    "provenance": "nameplate"
  },
  "sensor": {
    "sensor_id": "load-current",
    "quantity": "current",
    "unit": "ampere",
    "range_min": 0.0,
    "range_max": 100.0,
    "direction": "positive_is_load_draw",
    "calibration_ref": "sct013-100-2026-08-19-a"
  },
  "actuator": { … },
  "proof": { … }
}
```

`rated_capacity.provenance` is one of `nameplate`, `installer_measured`,
`design_document`. It is recorded because a capacity is a safety parameter and
an audit needs to know what kind of claim it was.

`direction` is explicit and has no default. A clamp mounted backwards reports a
hazard as a fall in magnitude, and a profile evaluating an upper bound would
never fire.

### Actuator

The actuator record carries an identity and a **commissioned outcome
mapping**. Every mapping field is an independently commissioned fact. None is
derived from another.

```json
{
  "kind": "local_gpio",
  "identity": { "gpio_pin": 26, "active_high": false },
  "commissioned_mapping": {
    "open_protected_circuit": "de_energised",
    "close_protected_circuit": "energised",
    "de_energised_terminal_state": "open"
  }
}
```

| Field | Values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open_protected_circuit` | `energised` \| `de_energised` | The coil state that isolates the load |
| `close_protected_circuit` | `energised` \| `de_energised` | The coil state that restores the load |
| `de_energised_terminal_state` | `open` \| `closed` | What the load does when the coil loses power |

**These three are checked against each other, never inferred from each other.**
A consumer MUST refuse a mapping in which the two outcomes name the same coil
state, or in which `de_energised_terminal_state` disagrees with whichever
outcome maps to `de_energised`. Checking declared facts for contradiction is
not derivation: all three are separately established by the proof, and
disagreement means the proof did not establish what the document records.

`active_high` is an electrical property of the driver stage: it maps a GPIO
level to a coil state. It says nothing about the load, and it is not a
substitute for any mapping field. It is required for `kind: local_gpio` and has
no default.

#### Actuator kinds

| `kind` | `identity` fields | Backend |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `local_gpio` | `gpio_pin` (int), `active_high` (bool) | Runtime-local GPIO |
| `firmware_channel` | `firmware_device_id` (string), `channel` (string) | Signed command per [firmware-commands/v1.md](../firmware-commands/v1.md) |

`kind` is a closed vocabulary. An unrecognised kind is refused, never treated
as the nearest known one.

#### Firmware Command Semantics

[firmware-commands/v1.md](../firmware-commands/v1.md) carries the actions
`relay_open` and `relay_close`. **These are protected-circuit outcomes.** They
are the wire spelling of this contract's `open_protected_circuit` and
`close_protected_circuit`, exactly:

| Wire action | Semantic outcome |
| --- | --- |
| `relay_open` | `open_protected_circuit` |
| `relay_close` | `close_protected_circuit` |

They are **not** coil operations and **not** contact states. This is already
what `ori-edge-firmware` D-021 requires — the relay driver maps `relay_open`
through the board profile to a coil state, so `relay_open` cannot itself name a
coil state, and because the map is per channel it cannot name a contact state
either. The vocabulary was ambiguous in the documents and unambiguous in the
code; this table closes the documents.

The names are retained rather than renamed. `firmware-commands/v1` is an
implemented baseline with a wire vocabulary closed at the producer (D-015), and
renaming a shipped action to improve a document is a breaking change bought
with no safety gain. A future `v2` may align the spellings; until then the
mapping above is normative and an implementation MUST NOT interpret the wire
names any other way.

#### Delivery Lifecycle For `firmware_channel`

**One route, and it is not runtime provisioning.** The commissioned mapping
reaches a firmware device as part of the image or the sealed provisioning
record, before the device is capable of actuating. It MUST NOT be delivered to
a running device as ordinary configuration.

A mapping that arrives over the network after boot creates a window in which
the device has actuation capability and no proven mapping. D-021 forbids a
default for exactly that window, so the device would have to refuse all
actuation until the mapping landed — which is either a safety gap or a startup
dependency on the network, and it makes the mapping's authority depend on the
transport that carries it.

The device's attested manifest therefore carries `binding_ref`: the
`binding_seq` and canonical hash of the binding its profile was derived from.
That is what makes disagreement detectable rather than silent — a runtime
holding a binding whose hash differs from the device's `binding_ref` knows the
two disagree without being able to see inside the firmware, and MUST refuse to
actuate that channel until they match. A firmware build whose profile
disagrees with the accepted binding is non-conforming.

Recommissioning a firmware channel is therefore a provisioning event, not a
configuration edit. That is the correct cost: rewiring a contactor is a site
visit already.

### Proof

```json
{
  "method": "actuate_and_observe",
  "performed_at_ms": 1800000000000,
  "observations": [
    {
      "commanded": "open_protected_circuit",
      "coil_state": "de_energised",
      "gpio_level": "high",
      "sensor_before": 6.4,
      "sensor_after": 0.02,
      "terminal_state_observed": "open"
    },
    {
      "commanded": "close_protected_circuit",
      "coil_state": "energised",
      "gpio_level": "low",
      "sensor_before": 0.02,
      "sensor_after": 6.4,
      "terminal_state_observed": "closed"
    }
  ]
}
```

| `method` | Establishes | Acceptable for autonomous actuation |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `actuate_and_observe` | Association, direction, and both terminal states, by measurement on the live circuit | Yes |
| `pre_energisation` | Terminal states at the terminals with a meter, and sensor association against a controlled test load, before the site load is first energised | Yes |
| `undemonstrated` | Nothing. Records that no proof was possible and why. | No |

A binding is signed only when its proof is complete for the method claimed. A
producer MUST NOT record a binding as proven that it did not demonstrate; an
`undemonstrated` zone carries a `reason` and no observations.

#### The proof is checked, not merely carried

A proof is not an attachment. **A consumer MUST verify that the recorded
observations agree with the mapping they claim to establish**, and refuse with
`proof_contradiction` when they do not. Specifically, for every observation:

- `coil_state` MUST equal the mapping's entry for `commanded`.
- `terminal_state_observed` MUST be `open` for `open_protected_circuit` and
  `closed` for `close_protected_circuit`.
- Where `sensor_after` is present, it MUST show load current consistent with
  `terminal_state_observed` — a circuit observed open while the sensor still
  reads load current is the transposition case.
- For `kind: local_gpio`, where `gpio_level` is present it MUST agree with
  `identity.active_high`: an energised coil is driven `high` when `active_high`
  is true and `low` when it is false. This is what makes a reversed driver
  stage detectable rather than asserted.
- Both outcomes MUST be observed. One-sided proof establishes one direction.

A proof whose observations contradict the mapping did not establish that
mapping, whoever signed it. This is what converts commissioning from an
assertion into a measurement, and it is what catches the three failures a
schema cannot: a transposed sensor and actuator, a reversed polarity, and a
sensor reading a different circuit. A signature proves authorship, not
correctness — which is exactly why authorship alone is insufficient here.

#### Undemonstrated bindings

**A zone whose proof method is `undemonstrated` MUST NOT activate a profile
whose action actuates.** In staging and production this is a hardened startup
failure, consistent with the missing-parameter rule in ori-specs#71. In
development posture the runtime starts with a consolidated WARNING and the
zone's actuating profiles inactive.

The zone still activates non-actuating profiles. Observation, alerting, and
Tier C proposal remain available on an unproven binding, because none of them
opens a circuit. Losing them as well would push installers toward recording a
proof they did not perform.

This is the resolution of the question ori-cli#34 defers to this contract. A
possibly-transposed autonomous cutoff is not safer than no autonomous cutoff:
it converts one hazard into two, and the second one arrives without warning on
a circuit nobody was watching.

## Bounds

Rated capacity is bounded by the declared hardware, so an implausible value
fails at the point a human can still see the wiring.

- `rated_capacity.value` MUST be greater than zero and MUST NOT exceed
  `sensor.range_max`. A capacity above the sensor's full scale describes a trip
  point the sensor cannot observe.
- Where a profile's condition multiplies capacity, the resulting trip point
  MUST also fall at or below `sensor.range_max`. A trip point beyond full scale
  is a cutoff that can never fire, and a saturating sensor reports it as a
  merely high reading.
- `range_min` MUST be less than `range_max`, and `value` MUST fall within the
  closed range.

These bounds do not replace the separate signer. A capacity within range is
still a safety parameter; the bound removes the absurd values, and the signer
separation removes the authority.

**The trip-point bound is not redundant with the capacity bound.** A 25 A
capacity on a 100 A clamp is unremarkable until a ×5 multiplier puts the trip
point at 125 A, past full scale, where a saturating sensor reports the hazard
as a merely high reading. The cutoff is then permanently unfireable while every
individual value looks reasonable. Authoring the reference vectors surfaced a
live instance of this: `ori.yaml.example` pairs a 10 A rated capacity with the
electrical overcurrent profile's ×5 multiplier, which on the 30 A clamp the
documentation recommends gives a 50 A trip point the sensor cannot reach.

## Disambiguation

Zones name hardware by identity, never by type.

- Two zones MUST NOT name the same actuator identity.
- Two zones MUST NOT name the same `sensor_id`.
- A `sensor_id` or actuator identity not present in the device's declared
  inventory is refused.
- A device with declared actuating hardware that no zone binds has no
  actuating profile for that hardware. This is a refusal in hardened posture,
  not a silent deactivation.

Ambiguity is refused rather than resolved. There is no nearest-match rule, no
ordering preference, and no single-candidate shortcut — a site with exactly one
clamp and one contactor still requires an explicit binding, because the next
revision of that site will have two.

## Change Lifecycle

Every change is a new binding document with `binding_seq` incremented and
`supersedes` naming the canonical hash of the one it replaces.

- A consumer MUST reject a binding whose `binding_seq` does not strictly exceed
  the accepted one, and whose `supersedes` does not match the accepted
  document's hash.
- The prior document is retained. A consumer MUST be able to produce the
  binding in force at any past time for audit.
- **Replacement, recalibration, rewiring, and polarity change all invalidate
  the proof.** A new actuator does not inherit the mapping the previous one was
  proven to have. A revision changing any actuator identity field, any mapping
  field, or `calibration_ref` MUST carry a fresh proof for that zone or record
  it as `undemonstrated`.
- A revision that changes only `rated_capacity` retains the proof but is
  re-bounds-checked.

## Delivery And Verification

A binding reaches the runtime as a signed document alongside the config, and
reaches firmware — for `firmware_channel` zones — as the source of the board
profile the device is built or provisioned with.

Verification order, fail-closed at each step:

1. Grammar. A shape violation is `malformed` and is rejected before signature
   verification.
2. `device_id` equals the consumer's own device.
3. Signature verifies against the commissioning trust anchor, supplied outside
   the document.
4. Signer is not the provisioning authority for this device.
5. Freshness: `binding_seq` and `supersedes`.
6. Self-consistency of every `commissioned_mapping`.
7. Proof consistency, including the revision rule below.
8. Bounds.
9. Disambiguation across zones.
10. Inventory: every named sensor and actuator exists, and every declared
    actuator is bound.
11. Activation posture: undemonstrated zones under hardened posture.

A binding failing any step is not partially applied. The previously accepted
binding remains in force unchanged.

**Order is normative, not incidental.** A refusal is only evidence that a
consumer performed a check if every earlier check passed, which is why the
golden corpus records the stage each rejection is decided at and requires that
no earlier stage fires first.

### Retained State

A consumer retains, from the binding in force, at least: `binding_seq`, the
canonical hash, and per zone the actuator identity, the commissioned mapping,
the calibration reference, and the time the proof was performed. Retention is
already required for audit; the revision rule reads the same record, so a
changed actuator cannot inherit the proof of the one it replaced.

### Canonical Bytes

Signatures are over the canonical serialisation of the `binding` object using
the canonical form of
[gateway-mqtt-canonical-json/v1.md](../gateway-mqtt-canonical-json/v1.md):
recursively sorted keys, no insignificant whitespace, RFC 8259 escaping,
caller-ordered arrays.

Numbers are additionally constrained to the D-011 agreement zone as required by
[evidence/v2.md](../evidence/v2.md) — integers within ±(2^53 − 1), non-integers
finite and either exactly zero or of magnitude in `[1e-4, 1e16)`, fixed
notation with a mandatory fractional part. The stricter zone applies here and
not on gateway telemetry because a C consumer participates in this contract:
firmware verifying its own board profile must produce the same bytes as a Go
producer and a Python runtime.

### Error Semantics

| Verdict | Decided at (stage name as recorded in the corpus) | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `malformed` | parses | Shape, grammar, unknown `kind`, unknown key, missing required field, number outside the agreement zone |
| `wrong_device` | device_id | `device_id` mismatch |
| `anchor_collision` | configuration load | A commissioning anchor and the provisioning anchor are the same key material |
| `wrong_authority` | authority | `signing_key` is the provisioning anchor |
| `superseded_signer` | authority | `signing_key` is the previous commissioning generation |
| `unknown_signer` | authority | `signing_key` is neither commissioning generation |
| `bad_signature` | signature | Signature does not verify against `signing_key` |
| `stale` | freshness | `binding_seq` not strictly increasing, or `supersedes` mismatch |
| `mapping_contradiction` | mapping_self_consistency | Mapping fields disagree with one another |
| `proof_contradiction` | proof_consistency | Observations disagree with the mapping they claim to establish |
| `stale_proof` | proof_consistency | A revision changed actuator identity, mapping, or calibration without a fresh proof |
| `out_of_bounds` | bounds | Capacity, trip point, or range violation |
| `ambiguous_binding` | disambiguation | Duplicate sensor or actuator identity across zones |
| `unknown_hardware` | inventory | Named sensor or actuator absent from declared inventory |
| `unbound_actuator` | inventory | Declared actuating hardware that no zone binds |
| `undemonstrated_binding` | activation_posture | An undemonstrated zone under hardened posture |

## Consumer Obligations

- A consumer MUST NOT actuate through an actuator whose zone has no accepted
  binding, and MUST NOT fall back to any default mapping when one is absent.
  Absence of a mapping is a refusal to actuate, never an assumption.
- A consumer MUST re-apply the mapping lookup at each actuation, not cache a
  resolved coil state across a binding revision.
- A consumer MUST record which `binding_seq` was in force for every actuation
  it logs, so an action can later be attributed to the physical arrangement it
  was taken under.
- Attestation quality never gates a trip. Where a binding is accepted and a
  reading is credible, the profile fires and any evidence gap is recorded.

### Golden Vectors

The authoritative corpus is
[binding-vectors-v1.json](binding-vectors-v1.json), held **here**, beside this
document. Its SHA-256 is
`fb08e348028692386cd2f38316f037e3eaa5ebac4b383aefcbb81f6f0affeb13`.

It carries four accept cases and twenty-four reject cases: one per verdict
above, plus the transposition, polarity, trip-point, rotation and
anchor-collision cases that motivate the rules. The reference site is two
clamps and two contactors whose zones carry **opposite** commissioned mappings,
so an implementation that derives one outcome from a convention rather than
reading it fails exactly one zone.

Implementing repositories copy the file byte-for-byte and pin that digest,
never generating its expectations from their own behaviour. The artifact lives
here rather than only its digest, for the reasons given in
[firmware-commands/v1.md](../firmware-commands/v1.md#golden-vectors): a CODEOWNER
asked to ratify a bare hash can only confirm that it is a hexadecimal number,
and two constants coordinated by hand are not a parity mechanism.

Every case records `canonical_hex`, `canonical_sha256` and `message_hex`, so a
producer's bytes are checkable without running a verifier, and a verifier's
verdicts are checkable without running a producer.

Reject cases carry two fields that make them meaningful:

- `reason` is the verdict, and `acceptance_order` fixes the stage it must be
  decided at. A case that refuses for the right reason at the wrong stage is
  not evidence that the named check exists.
- `signature_valid` distinguishes a refusal decided before the signature check
  from one decided after it. A binding, freshness or bounds refusal only
  demonstrates that check ran if the signature was good.

**Ratification requires both halves.** The corpus is not ratified until a
generator reproduces the file byte-for-byte and an **independent verifier, in a
different language from the generator, and not sharing its code**, accepts
every accept case and refuses every reject case at its declared stage. A corpus
checked only by the tool that wrote it certifies whatever that tool does,
including its defects.

Both artifacts exist for this corpus and are the reason four defects were
caught before ratification, including a reference site whose own trip point sat
beyond its sensor's full scale. They are named under Required Implementation
Artifacts and land with the first implementing repository; `ori-specs` is
documentation-only and holds neither.

## Open Questions

- **Commissioning key custody.** Issuance, rotation, and revocation of the
  commissioning key are not settled here. ori-cli#34 explicitly excludes it.
  Until settled, a deployment has no defined recovery from a lost or
  compromised commissioning key, and this contract does not pretend otherwise.
- **Multi-device zones.** A zone whose sensor is on the runtime host and whose
  actuator is a firmware channel is expressible above, but the failure
  semantics when the firmware device is unreachable at binding-verification
  time are not specified.
- **Re-proof cadence.** Whether a proof expires — and what a site does about a
  binding proven three years ago — is unspecified. Wiring changes without a
  document change are outside what any signature can detect.
- **Independence of the second implementation.** Both current vector tools are
  Python. Until a verifier exists in another language, the corpus demonstrates
  internal consistency rather than cross-language agreement, which is the
  property the canonical-bytes rules exist for.
