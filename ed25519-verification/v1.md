# Ed25519 Verification Contract v1

> Status: maturity `draft`; compatibility `amendable`; adoption: none
> recorded; proof: end-to-end `none`, HIL `none`, production `none`. Record:
> [status/ed25519-verification/v1.json](../status/ed25519-verification/v1.json).

This contract decides which Ed25519 signatures an Ori verifier accepts. It
names one verification equation and one decoding of the signature, so that a
device and its runtime, the evidence authority, the CLI and the SDK reach the
same verdict on the same signed bytes. Which key may verify is
[ed25519-key-admission/v1](../ed25519-key-admission/v1.md)'s; the encoding a
signature travels in, the bytes it signs and the rejection reason a refusal is
reported under are each the citing contract's.

## Why one equation

RFC 8032, section 5.1.7, lets a verifier check either the cofactored equation,
[8][S]B = [8]R + [8][k]A, or the cofactorless one, [S]B = R + [k]A.
Separately, implementations differ in how strictly they decode R and A: RFC
8032, section 5.1.3, refuses a y of p or more, a point off the curve, and x = 0
with bit 255 set, and some libraries accept one or more of these. The choices
disagree on
signatures that only a private key holder can make: R with a component of
small order, R of small order, and R in an encoding other than the canonical
one. A verifier taking the cofactored equation with loose decoding accepts a
strict superset of what a strict cofactorless verifier accepts. A signer can
therefore send a message one Ori verifier acts on and another, auditing the
same bytes later, judges invalid.

None of these signatures can be made without the private key, and replay is
unaffected, because freshness never keys on signature bytes. What
the disagreement breaks is non-repudiation: the device and the auditor must
agree on which signed bytes a key holder said.

This contract takes the cofactorless equation with strict decoding, because
OpenSSL, ring, Go's `crypto/ed25519` and ed25519-dalek's `verify` already
implement it, and none of the first three has a cofactored mode. Inspecting
R's encoding cannot make a cofactored verifier agree: a cofactored equation
accepts R = [r]B + T for any point T of small order, and that R is a canonical
encoding of a curve point.

## Scope

A contract cites this one for every Ed25519 signature it verifies: a command,
an approval, a liveness message, a telemetry envelope, a fault, a signed
document. A citing contract adopts every rule here for those signatures. It
still states how the 64 signature bytes and the 32 key bytes are carried, the
exact message bytes signed, and which of its own rejection reasons a refused
signature is reported under.

Only PureEdDSA over edwards25519, `Ed25519` in RFC 8032, section 5.1, is in
scope: no context string and no prehash. `Ed25519ctx` and `Ed25519ph` are not
Ori signature algorithms.

## Inputs

A verification takes a public key A of 32 bytes, a message M of any length
and a signature of 64 bytes: R, the first 32, and S, the last 32 read as a
little-endian integer. A citing contract refuses a key or a signature of any
other length in its carriage, before this contract applies.

p is 2^255 − 19, B is the base point of RFC 8032, section 5.1, and L is the
order of B, 2^252 + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493. Points are
encoded and decoded as in RFC 8032, sections 5.1.2 and 5.1.3.

## Refusal clauses

A signature is refused under the first of these clauses that holds, in this
order, and is accepted when none holds:

1. `key_refused`: ed25519-key-admission/v1 refuses A, under any of its
   clauses. The signature is not examined.
2. `s_out_of_range`: S ≥ L. S is not reduced modulo L to make it fit.
3. `non_canonical_r`: R is not the encoding of any curve point under RFC 8032,
   section 5.1.2: its y is p or more, it is on no curve point, or its x is 0
   and bit 255 is set.
4. `equation`: with k the SHA-512 digest of R ‖ A ‖ M read as a little-endian
   integer, the encoding of [S]B − [k]A is not equal to R, byte for byte.

k is the full 512-bit integer, as RFC 8032, section 5.1.7, specifies. Under
every key ed25519-key-admission/v1 admits, A has order L, so [k]A equals
[k mod L]A and an implementation that reduces k modulo L first reaches the
same verdict. The rule is stated in full so that it does not depend on that.

Clause 4 compares bytes, not points. Encoding is canonical, so a byte compare
refuses every R that clause 3 refuses. Clause 3 is stated so that a verifier
which decodes R and compares points reaches the same verdict, and so that the
reason is reported where an implementation reports reasons.

Clause 4 does not refuse an R of small order: a signature whose R is the
identity and whose S is k·a, the private scalar a times k, is accepted. Only
the holder of a makes one, and making it discloses a. Any other R of small
order, and any R with a component of small order, fails the equation, because
[S]B − [k]A is in the subgroup of order L when A is admitted.

The clause names are this contract's and are what its corpus records. An
implementation need not report them, but the set of signatures it accepts is
exactly the set accepted here. The order fixes only which clause a
reason-reporting implementation names. A citing contract may refuse further
signatures as policy, and those refusals are that contract's.

## Implementations

A cofactored verifier, or one that decodes R or A loosely, does not conform.
A cofactored verifier reaches this contract's verdicts only if it also decodes
R strictly and refuses an R for which [L]R is not the identity, at the cost of
a further scalar multiplication; with A admitted, S < L and R in the subgroup
of order L, the two equations then agree. Replacing it with a cofactorless
verifier with strict decoding is the simpler route, and the corpus is the test
either way.

A verifier whose library refuses an R of small order, such as
ed25519-dalek's `verify_strict`, refuses the identity-R signature this
contract accepts and does not conform. Its other refusals, of small-order and
non-canonical keys, are ed25519-key-admission/v1's, which an implementation
applies itself.

Neither OpenSSL, ring, Go's `crypto/ed25519` nor ed25519-dalek's `verify`
applies ed25519-key-admission/v1, and each accepts three of the four
`refused_keys` signatures. Each conforms only behind its own admission check.

> Implementation: no implementation claims conformance to this contract. The
> corpus below is the conformance target.

## Relation to other contracts

- ed25519-key-admission/v1 decides which keys verify. This contract cites it
  for A and decides which signatures an admitted key accepts.
- `firmware-commands/v2` and `firmware-telemetry/v2` cite this contract for
  every signature they verify, and ed25519-key-admission/v1 for every key they
  admit. Their v1 versions are frozen and are not amended.
- `device-provisioning/v1` and `firmware-mqtt-provisioning/v1` are frozen and
  are not amended. The successor of each MUST cite this contract for every
  Ed25519 signature it verifies.
- The same applies to the successor of any other contract that verifies an
  Ed25519 signature, including `evidence-exchange`, `offline-tokens`,
  `runtime-config-signing`, `commissioned-safety-binding`,
  `runtime-release-bundle` and `signing`.

No signature RFC 8032, section 5.1.6, produces is refused by any clause, so
honest signers are unaffected. The rule still changes which inputs a
cofactored verifier rejects, so it arrives in each of those contracts as
successor semantics. An implementation of a frozen version that applies it
ahead of that successor rejects inputs the frozen version accepts. That is
hardening, not conformance to either contract, and its status record says so.

## Corpus

[`vectors-v1.json`](vectors-v1.json) carries one case per signature, each a
public key, a message, a signature, the expected verdict and, when refused,
the clause that refuses it, in nine sections:

- `rfc8032`: RFC 8032, section 7.1, TEST 1 to 3, accepted.
- `honest`: signatures under a seed-derived key with R under both sign bits,
  accepted, and one of them over another message and under another admitted
  key, each refused under `equation`.
- `negated_r`: R is the negation of the honest nonce point, its bit 255
  flipped, and S = r + k·a, under both sign bits. [S]B − [k]A then differs
  from R in bit 255 alone; each is refused under `equation`.
- `flipped_bit_r`: for each bit 0 to 254, R is the honest nonce point with that
  bit flipped, chosen so that R is still a canonical point of order L, and
  S = r + k·a. [S]B − [k]A then differs from R in that bit alone, so with
  `negated_r` a verifier whose final comparison omits any one bit or byte of R
  accepts one of these; each is refused under `equation`.
- `torsion_component_r`: for each of the seven nonzero points T of small
  order, R = [r]B + T and S = r + k·a. The cofactored equation accepts each;
  each is refused under `equation`.
- `small_order_r`: R is each of the eight points of small order and S = k·a.
  The cofactored equation accepts each. The identity is accepted; the other
  seven are refused under `equation`.
- `non_canonical_r`: every encoding that a decoder reducing y modulo p, or
  ignoring the sign of x = 0, maps to a point of small order other than by its
  canonical encoding, with S = k·a, which such a decoder with the cofactored
  equation accepts, and an R on no curve point under both sign bits. Each is
  refused under `non_canonical_r`.
- `s_range`: an honest signature with L added to S, S = L, S = 2^256 − 1, and
  S ≥ L with a non-canonical R. Each is refused under `s_out_of_range`.
- `refused_keys`: the identity and the point of order 2 as keys, and a
  seed-derived key shifted by a point of order 8, each with a signature the
  cofactorless equation accepts with the full and with the reduced challenge,
  and the identity key with S ≥ L. Each is refused under `key_refused`.

`scripts/check-ed25519-verification-vectors` recomputes every verdict and
clause from the rules above with its own affine curve arithmetic, derives the
points of small order rather than reading them, restates the RFC 8032
signatures, and holds each section to the property that makes it
differentiating: the cofactored equation accepts every torsion and small-order
case, a loose decoder with the cofactored equation accepts every non-canonical
alias, an honest signature verifies once its S is reduced, the candidate
differs from R in exactly the declared bit for every negated-R and
flipped-bit case, every bit appearing once, and the
cofactorless equation accepts every refused-key signature with S < L. With
`--mutations` it requires every corrupted corpus, every rule switched off or
reordered, and a final comparison omitting each of the 32 bytes and each of
the 256 bits of R in turn to fail for its named reason. A comparison that
omits several positions at once is caught too, because every position is the
only one some case differs in.

`scripts/generate-ed25519-verification-vectors` produces the corpus from fixed
seeds and messages, shares no code with the checker, and requires OpenSSL to
reproduce the RFC 8032 signatures and to give every case whose key is
admitted its expected verdict. CI requires the committed corpus to be exactly
what it produces, so the corpus is never edited by hand.
