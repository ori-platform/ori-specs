# Supply Transfer Contract v1

> Status: Design target, pre-ratification. No shipped consumer claims
> conformance, no corpus exists, and no implementation is started. Amendable
> in place under the
> [pre-ratification exception](../VERSIONING.md#pre-ratification-exception).
>
> **This contract is not implementable in its current state.** It names an
> outcome the commissioned binding contract cannot yet describe, and its
> transition bounds are unratified — see [Bounds](#bounds). A consumer that
> commands a supply transfer today has authored the safety facts this document
> has not yet decided. The rule is normative, not advisory.

A supply transfer moves a load from one source to another: grid to generator,
generator to inverter, inverter to grid. Four skills that shipped with the
runtime asked for it by name — `switch_to_grid`, `switch_to_generator`,
`switch_to_inverter`, and a generic `switch_power_source` — and every one of
those names was retired from the runtime's action registry, because none of
them could be made true as a name. The demand is real. The vocabulary does not
exist, and this contract is where it is defined before anything commands one.

## Why This Contract Exists

[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md) holds this open by name: *a
profile that switches a supply rather than isolating a load would need an
outcome vocabulary and a commissioned mapping shape this contract and the
binding contract do not define.* The outcome vocabulary that exists describes
a protected circuit with two states, and a transfer is not one circuit opening.

A transfer describes at least three things the binding contract cannot carry
today: the identity of each source, the exclusion between them, and the
transition — what is de-energised in between, for how long, and what the load
sees during it. Its safe operation depends on interlocks that are properties of
the switchgear and its commissioning, not of a coil state. Naming it as an
outcome without a zone shape that records those facts would let the runtime
command a transition whose safety nobody has established.

So this contract does three things and refuses a fourth. It defines the
outcome. It defines what a zone must record before that outcome may be resolved
on it. It states which authority may ever request it. And it does not define a
profile that commands it, because a transfer that restores supply is the one
outcome the safety-profile contract forbids a profile to name.

## Two Different Things Are Called Switching

| Term | Outcome | Contract | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Isolation** | `open_protected_circuit` | [safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md) | The load or supply is disconnected from its source and stays so |
| **Transfer** | `transfer_supply` | This contract | The load is disconnected from one source and connected to another |

They are not the same act with different arguments. Isolating a faulty source
is `open_protected_circuit` on that source's zone, already expressible, already
the protective outcome. A transfer has a destination, and a destination is what
makes it a different consequence: the load is energised again at the end of it,
from a source whose availability the runtime did not establish. A transfer is
never a spelling of isolation with no destination, and `transfer_supply` with
no `to` is refused rather than read as one.

## Vocabulary

| Layer | Owned by | Example values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | This contract | `transfer_supply` |
| Source | The zone, per [commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md) | `grid`, `generator`, `inverter` |
| Selection | The zone's commissioned mapping | Which selector state connects which source |
| Transition | The zone, from its proof | `break_before_make`, dead time, intermediate state |
| Resting state | The zone, from its proof | Where a transfer that fails part-way leaves the load |

A request names an outcome and a destination source. It never names a selector
state, a relay, a contactor, or a transition parameter. Those are the zone's.

## The Outcome

```json
{ "outcome": "transfer_supply", "to": "generator" }
```

`transfer_supply` is the only parameterised outcome in the estate, and `to` is
its only parameter. It resolves on a zone whose actuator kind is
`supply_transfer` and whose declared sources include `to`; a destination the
zone does not declare is refused before anything is commanded. Two requests to
the same zone with different destinations are different acts, and a request
naming the source currently connected is refused as a no-op rather than
performed — a transfer that changes nothing still opens the intermediate state.

## What A Zone Must Record

The actuator record for a transfer zone carries an identity, the declared
sources, the selection mapping, the transition, and the resting state. Every
field is an independently commissioned fact established by the proof; none is
derived from another, and a consumer refuses a record whose fields contradict
each other rather than reconciling them.

```json
{
  "kind": "supply_transfer",
  "identity": { "…": "actuator-specific, closed per kind" },
  "sources": [
    { "source_id": "grid", "role": "primary" },
    { "source_id": "generator", "role": "standby" }
  ],
  "exclusive": true,
  "selection_mapping": {
    "grid": "…",
    "generator": "…"
  },
  "transition": {
    "sequence": "break_before_make",
    "dead_time_ms": { "min": 0, "max": 0 },
    "intermediate_state": "no_source"
  },
  "resting_state_on_failure": "no_source"
}
```

| Field | Values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sources[].source_id` | string, unique in the zone | What a request may name as `to` |
| `sources[].role` | `primary` \| `standby` \| `declared` | Commissioned description; confers no authority |
| `exclusive` | `true` | At most one source is connected at any instant, and exactly one in every stable energised state — the `no_source` interval of a break-before-make transition is the only time none is. `false` is refused |
| `selection_mapping` | per source, actuator-specific | The selector state that connects that source and no other |
| `transition.sequence` | `break_before_make` | The only sequence v1 admits. Make-before-break is refused: it is the case whose safety this contract cannot bound |
| `transition.dead_time_ms` | bounded range, **unratified** | How long the load is without a source during a transfer |
| `transition.intermediate_state` | `no_source` | What the load sees between sources. v1 admits no other value |
| `resting_state_on_failure` | a `source_id` \| `no_source` | Where a transfer that fails part-way leaves the load, established by the proof |

**The identity is actuator-specific and closed per kind.** A transfer switch is
not a relay with more positions; what identifies it — a transfer-switch
controller, a set of interlocked contactors, a firmware channel that owns the
sequencing — is decided when a kind is added, and this contract does not
enumerate kinds it has no proof method for.

**The proof has more legs than a circuit.** A protected-circuit zone proves a
circuit leg and a control leg. A transfer zone must additionally prove, for
every ordered pair of declared sources, that the transition sequence is what
the record says, that the dead time falls inside the recorded bound, that the
intermediate state is the one recorded, and that the resting state on a
failure induced during the transition is the one recorded. A pair not proven
is a transfer the zone does not permit. The method by which those are observed
and attested is not defined here and is the first thing this contract needs.

## Authority

**`transfer_supply` is never a safety-profile outcome in v1.** The safety
profile contract reserves `close_protected_circuit` to local arm and reset and
forbids any profile from naming it, because a profile that restores supply
autonomously has removed the operator from the one decision where the
operator's absence is not a safety argument. A transfer ends with the load
energised from a source the runtime chose. That is the same shape, and it is
refused for the same reason.

A transfer has one authority model, and it does not vary with the trigger:

```text
trigger tier          A, B or C — observes the condition, confers no action authority
transfer action floor always C — hard consequence on every zone
execution authority   Tier C operator approval, with the destination named in the
                      proposal the operator answers
post_action           never — a soft action that reverses itself after the fact
                      does not describe a load that was without a source in between
autonomous            never — not a safety outcome; see above
```

A Tier A or Tier B trigger may *propose* a transfer; the transfer itself
dispatches at C under the Tier C approval workflow and never executes at the
trigger's tier. The consequence class is `hard` on every zone in v1, whatever
the zone says about reversibility: the intermediate state is a load without a
source, and a transition that can be reversed is not one that did not happen.
A package may not declare the floor lower.

**`transfer_supply` is not requestable today.** After
[skills-package/v3.md](../skills-package/v3.md#physical-requests-are-outcomes-on-zones),
`open_protected_circuit` is the only outcome a package may request, and a
package naming `transfer_supply` is refused as any outcome outside that
vocabulary is. Adding it is a later amendment to that contract, made only after
the zone shape, the proof method, the three ceilings below and the evidence
representation are settled — and that amendment is listed as a prerequisite in
the artifacts table. Until it lands, nothing may request a transfer, and this
contract's authority model is what that amendment will carry rather than a
permission that exists now.

## Failure

A transfer has three ends and a consumer must be able to tell them apart:

| Outcome | Load is at | Reported as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed | the destination source | `transfer_supply` executed, destination named |
| Refused before the transition | the origin source, unchanged | refused, with the reason |
| Failed during the transition | `resting_state_on_failure` | **the commissioned resting state**, whether that is `no_source` or a named source — never the origin or destination inferred from which command was last sent |

The third row is the one that matters. A transfer that failed part-way has left
the load wherever the switchgear rests, and that is a commissioned fact the
zone recorded, not something the runtime infers from which command it last
sent. The rule is about inference, not about which state is reported: if the
zone's proof established that a failed transition rests on the origin source,
the origin source is the honest report, because it was observed rather than
assumed. A consumer that reports a failed transfer as "still on grid" because
that was the last known state, or "now on generator" because that was the last
command sent, has asserted a supply state it did not observe.

A command that does not return within its timeout is **uncertain**, with the
same meaning it has for a protected-circuit outcome: neither completed nor
failed, no opposing or repeated transfer admitted on that zone while it stands,
and the load's state reported as unverified until the driver reports.

## Evidence

A transfer is a physical act at Tier C and is sealed under
[evidence/v2.md](../evidence/v2.md) like any other. What that row must carry
beyond the licence — the origin source, the destination, whether the
intermediate state was observed, and where the load rested if the transfer
failed — is not representable in the `runtime_action` payload today. It is an
amendment to that contract, raised when this one is close enough to
implementation to know the exact fields, and not before.

## Bounds

Three ceilings are unratified, and a consumer MUST refuse to command a
transfer while any of them is:

- **Dead time.** The bound on `transition.dead_time_ms` inside which a
  transfer is admissible. It is a property of the load — what a refrigeration
  compressor, a server, or a pump tolerates without a source — and it is a
  commissioning fact, not a release constant. The zone records a measured range;
  what range is acceptable for what load is the ceiling.
- **Rate.** How many transfers a zone may perform in a window, because a
  transfer is a wear event on switchgear and a stress event on the load, and a
  reasoning loop that oscillates between sources is a failure mode this
  contract must make impossible rather than unlikely.
- **Source availability.** What a zone must know about a destination before
  connecting the load to it. A transfer to a generator that is not running is a
  transfer to no source with extra steps. Whether availability is a commissioned
  interlock, a measured fact the zone reports, or both, is undecided.

## Golden Vectors

None. A corpus for this contract would vector a zone shape the binding contract
does not define and ceilings that are not ratified; every case in it would be
deferred, and a deferred case is not coverage.

## Open Questions

- **Whether v1 admits more than two sources.** Every shipped request named a
  pair. A three-source zone multiplies the proof legs and makes the resting
  state a function of which transition failed; whether that is worth carrying
  in v1 or is a v2 concern is undecided.
- **Whether a transfer may ever be autonomous.** This contract says never. The
  case for reconsidering it is a load whose loss of source is itself a hazard
  and whose standby is proven available — which is a safety condition with a
  restoring outcome, and the profile contract's reason for refusing that is not
  that it is never right but that no argument for it has been made.
- **What "available" means for a source.** See Bounds.
- **Whether a transfer's approval proposal must name the dead time.** An
  operator approving a transfer is approving a period without a source; whether
  the proposal states it, and in what unit, is a decision about what the
  operator is consenting to.
