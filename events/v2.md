# Event Schema v2

> Status: maturity `draft`; compatibility `amendable`; adoption: none
> recorded; proof: end-to-end `none`, HIL `none`, production `none`. Record:
> [status/events/v2.json](../status/events/v2.json).
>
> [events/v1.md](v1.md) remains the record every consumer reads. The Tier C approval
> record below is a **proposal awaiting ratification**. Its field names, types
> and values are not ratified, and no implementation may claim them.


This version exists for one breaking change: what a Tier C action record says
about an operator's approval once approval is a durable admission, per
[tier-c-approval/v1.md](../tier-c-approval/v1.md).

## Relationship to v1

`events/v1` retains its published approval semantics. In v1, `approved=true`
means an operator affirmative token and `approved=false` means an explicit NO,
a timeout or no response, or an unrecognized reply; a consumer of v1 reads
`approved=false` as the operator not approving.

Admission makes those two facts separate: an operator can answer YES and the
reply still not become an approval, because its commit failed, the proposal
expired, the commissioned binding or authority changed, or the process
restarted. Redefining `approved` in place would give a v1 consumer v2 meaning
without telling it. A v1 consumer is never given v2 semantics: a v1 field keeps
its v1 meaning in every record that carries it, and v2 approval semantics are
carried only in fields v1 does not define.

Except for the Tier C approval record expressly defined here, events/v2
incorporates the definitions of events/v1 unchanged. This incorporation does not
assign v2 semantics to any v1 record or field.

A host-state Tier C action, which stays on the existing approval workflow
outside [tier-c-approval/v1.md](../tier-c-approval/v1.md), stays on events/v1
semantics until a resource-scoped approval contract exists. Its record keeps
the v1 `approved` field with its v1 meaning, and no events/v2 approval record
is emitted for it.

`scripts/check-tier-c-approval-vectors` holds [events/v1.md](v1.md) to its
published approval sentences and refuses any term only v2 defines there. This
guard proves only that the published v1 prose was not edited and that proposed
v2 vocabulary did not enter v1. It does not prove wire compatibility or
consumer behavior. Before events/v2 ratification or implementation, a data
vector and independent v1/v2 consumer checks are mandatory.

## Proposal: Tier C approval record (awaiting ratification)

The fields and values in this section are a sketch for review, and each is
open until ratified.

| Field | Type | Proposed meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `operator_reply` | `string \| null` | The authenticated operator reply as classified: `affirmative`, `negative` or `unrecognized`; `null` when no authenticated reply arrived before the proposal closed |
| `approval_admitted` | `bool` | Whether an affirmative reply was durably admitted as an approval |
| `decision_state` | `string` | The proposal's state, from the closed set in [tier-c-approval/v1.md](../tier-c-approval/v1.md) |
| `approved_action_outcome` | `string` | What is known of the approved action: `not_dispatched`, `executed`, `dispatch_failed`, `unknown`, `reconciled_executed` or `reconciled_not_executed` |
| `safe_default_outcome` | `string` | Whether the non-actuating safe default ran: `not_applied`, `executed` or `failed` |

### Tier C Semantics

- `approval_admitted=true`: an operator affirmative reply durably admitted as
  an approval. A reply whose admission commit did not succeed is not an
  approval, and admission, dispatch, expiry and restart follow
  [tier-c-approval/v1.md](../tier-c-approval/v1.md).
- `approval_admitted=false`: explicit NO, timeout/no response, a reply after
  expiry, a reply whose admission commit failed, a reply recorded
  `approval_binding_changed`, a proposal closed `proposal_aborted_restart`, or
  an unrecognized reply.
- Consumers MUST NOT infer an operator rejection from `approval_admitted=false`.
  Only `operator_reply=negative` records that the operator said no.
- A proposal closed `proposal_aborted_restart` or `approval_binding_changed`
  keeps `approval_admitted=false`; its `approved_action_outcome` is
  `not_dispatched`, and `safe_default_outcome` reports the proposal's one
  safe-default intent.
- Proposed, awaiting ratification: a proposal closed
  `proposal_blocked_uncertain_outcome` keeps `approval_admitted=false`; its
  `approved_action_outcome` is `not_dispatched`, and `safe_default_outcome`
  reports the proposal's one safe-default intent.
- An approval that was admitted and then recorded
  `approval_expired_undispatched` or `approval_aborted_undispatched` keeps
  `approval_admitted=true`; its `approved_action_outcome` is `not_dispatched`,
  and `safe_default_outcome` reports only whether that safe default ran. The
  approved action did not run.
- An approval recorded `dispatch_outcome_unknown` or `dispatch_not_proven`
  keeps `approval_admitted=true`, and its `approved_action_outcome` is
  `unknown`: no record reports that the approved action ran or did not until a
  reconciliation records `reconciled_executed` or `reconciled_not_executed`,
  which is appended and never rewrites the uncertain record.
- Proposed, awaiting ratification: a proposal still `proposed` has
  `approval_admitted=false`, its `approved_action_outcome` is `not_dispatched`,
  and its `safe_default_outcome` is `not_applied`.
- Proposed, awaiting ratification: a proposal recorded `proposal_expired` or
  `rejected` keeps `approval_admitted=false`; its `approved_action_outcome` is
  `not_dispatched`, and `safe_default_outcome` reports whether the
  non-actuating safe default ran.
- Proposed, awaiting ratification: an approval recorded
  `dispatch_refused_contention` keeps `approval_admitted=true`; its
  `approved_action_outcome` is `not_dispatched`, because the dispatcher refused
  the approved act before actuation, and `safe_default_outcome` reports the
  non-actuating safe default the runtime then applies.
- `safe_default_outcome` is independent of `approval_admitted`: a safe default
  runs after a NO as after a timeout.
