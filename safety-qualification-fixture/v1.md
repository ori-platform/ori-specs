# Safety Qualification Fixture Contract v1

> Status: Design target, pre-ratification. No shipped consumer claims
> conformance. A **partial corpus** is authored: the withdrawal document in
> full, and the fixture document's ceiling-independent verdicts as **deferred**
> cases that are not coverage — see [Golden Vectors](#golden-vectors).
> Amendable in place under the
> [pre-ratification exception](../VERSIONING.md#pre-ratification-exception).
>
> **This contract is not implementable in its current state.** Its three
> exposure ceilings are unratified, so a conforming consumer refuses every
> fixture — see [Bounds](#bounds). The rule is normative, not advisory: a
> consumer that accepts a fixture today has authored the safety bounds this
> document has not yet decided.

Every profile in the v1 safety profile set is a `candidate`: it activates
nothing, evaluates nothing, and executes nothing, because a number that
reproduces a legacy trigger has been shown equivalent to it and not shown safe.
Ratification needs evidence that cannot be gathered without running the
condition against real hardware, and running it against real hardware requires
the very authority ratification withholds.

This contract defines the one bounded, release-owned instrument that breaks
that circle: a **qualification fixture**, a signed artifact naming one device,
one binding, one zone and one candidate profile, which permits that single pair
to evaluate and actuate for a bounded supervised session, and whose product is
evidence toward a later ratification review — never a protection claim, and
never a ratification.

## Why This Contract Exists

[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md) is deliberately absolute: a
consumer MUST NOT activate a candidate on any zone, MUST NOT evaluate it, and
MUST NOT execute its outcome, and MUST NOT carry a local override that treats a
candidate as ratified. That rule is what stops a bench demonstration, an
installer's convenience flag, or a configuration switch from turning an
unratified number into a live cutoff.

The rule is right and it is also complete enough to prevent the work that would
retire it. Three of the open questions blocking `electrical.overcurrent.v1` can
only be closed by observation on a commissioned circuit: whether the declared
`rated_capacity_amps` is the quantity that limits the circuit, whether the
measurement path reports amperes the evaluator can be compared against
([ori-runtime#398](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-runtime/issues/398)),
and whether the pre-command intent-journal deadline is tolerable for the
hazard. None of them is answerable from a host test.

Three ways of getting that evidence were available and two are refused here.

**Ratifying the profile first** inverts the argument: it makes the number
authoritative on every device holding the release in order to test it on one.
**A local override** — a flag, an environment variable, a development-posture
exemption, a monkeypatched profile set — is the exact mechanism the safety
profile contract forbids, and its blast radius is every device whose operator
can set it. What remains is an artifact that is **signed by the release
authority, addressed to one device, and useless anywhere else**, which is the
shape defined below.

A fixture is a grant of Tier D authority to an unratified condition. That is
stated plainly rather than softened: it is bounded in device, binding, zone,
profile, time, and session count, it requires a second independent act by a
human at the installation, and it is reported as unprotected throughout. Those
bounds are what make it acceptable. They are not what make it harmless.

## Two Different Things Are Called Qualification

| Term | Contract | Establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Safety qualification fixture** | This contract | That one candidate safety profile behaved as specified on one commissioned zone, under supervision |
| **Actuator-platform qualification** | [ori-specs#158](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-specs/issues/158), not yet written | What an actuator platform's outputs do under process loss, kernel failure, and reset |

Neither substitutes for the other and neither is an input to the other. A
fixture session qualifies no platform, and a platform qualification ratifies no
profile. A consumer MUST NOT report one as evidence of the other.

The [commissioning proof operation](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md#the-commissioning-proof-operation)
is a third thing again. It proves the control leg of a **provisional binding**
by commanding a coil whose polarity is still an assertion. This contract acts
only on a binding already **in force**, whose control and circuit legs are both
proven, and it exercises a **profile** over that binding rather than the binding
itself. A consumer MUST NOT accept a fixture against a provisional binding.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Fixture | The signed artifact defined by this contract |
| Pair | One `(zone_id, profile_id)` combination, as in [safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md) |
| Accepted | The fixture passed every verification stage; no session is implied |

| Qualification record | The local evidence artifact a session produces |
| Ratification | A change to `profiles.json` flipping a profile's `status`; never performed here |

## Scope And Trust Model

**A fixture confers no authority the release does not already hold.** The
release owns Tier D; a fixture is the release exercising its own authority on
one device it designates, for a bounded time, over a condition it has not yet
ratified. It is not a delegation to a site, an installer, an operator, or a
tool.

| Party | May | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Release / qualification authority | Issue a fixture for a designated device, binding, zone and profile | Ratify a profile by issuing one; issue one that alters a profile parameter |
| Commissioning authority | Supply the zone the fixture names, through a binding in force | Sign a fixture; select, extend, or widen one |
| Provisioning authority | Declare the hardware | Sign a fixture; supply any fixture field |
| Local operator | Consent to open a session; arm; reset; end a session | Create, edit, extend, or re-address a fixture |
| Skill, DevicePolicy, entitlement, remote command, gateway, fleet API | Nothing | Deliver, select, alter, open, extend, or observe-and-act-on a fixture |

**Nothing gates a fixture and a fixture gates nothing.** Its acceptance has no
dependency on an entitlement, a licence, a subscription, payment state,
evidence availability, or connectivity, for the reasons
[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#scope-and-trust-model) gives; and
its absence never gates a ratified profile. A ratified profile activates on an
eligible zone with no fixture present, and a fixture naming a ratified profile
is refused rather than treated as a no-op.

### The Qualification Anchor

The trust root is the **qualification-authority Ed25519 key**, a distinct key
purpose held by the party that owns the release.

| Anchor | Source | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `qualification_anchor_current` | `ORI_QUALIFICATION_ANCHOR_PUBLIC_KEY_B64` in the service environment the installer writes | The generation that may issue new fixtures |
| `qualification_anchor_previous` | `ORI_QUALIFICATION_ANCHOR_PREVIOUS_PUBLIC_KEY_B64`, optional | The superseded generation, verify-only |

Both hold the canonical base64 of the raw 32-byte Ed25519 public key. The
variable names are constants of this contract and of the installer. No artifact
of the configuration set names, selects, or overrides them, for the reason
[runtime-config/v2](../runtime-config/v2.md) retires every anchor selector: a
document that could choose the key that authorises it authorises itself.
Absence of the current anchor with a fixture present is `unknown_signer`,
because no key can be selected.

**The separation from every other anchor is structural.** A consumer MUST
refuse at configuration load, with `anchor_collision`, when either
qualification anchor equals the provisioning anchor, either commissioning
anchor, or the config-signing anchor as raw bytes. If the commissioning key
could sign a fixture, an installer certifying an installation could also decide
which unratified condition that installation fires on, which is precisely the
separation [commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md#scope-and-trust-model)
exists to hold. If the provisioning key could, the commercial plane could.

Key **selection** happens before the signature; every **authority verdict**
happens after it. The stages, and the reason they are separate, are identical
to the binding contract's
[key selection rules](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md#key-selection-generations-and-rotation)
and are not restated here. Rotation follows the same posture: previous
verifies, current issues.

## The One Lever

**A fixture lifts exactly one refusal, for exactly one pair, for the duration
of an open session.** During that window, and only for the pair the fixture
names, the second check of the
[activation order](../safety-profile/v1.md#activation) —
`profile.status == ratified`, refusing with `pending_ratification` — is
satisfied instead by an accepted fixture with an open session.

Every other check in that order is evaluated unchanged and refuses exactly as
it otherwise would: quantity eligibility, `unit_mismatch`,
`direction_unsupported`, `parameter_mismatch`, `trip_point_unobservable`,
`undemonstrated_binding`. A fixture rescues none of them. It is not a
suppression of activation refusals; it is the substitution of one specific
authority test.

Nothing else changes. In particular a fixture MUST NOT:

- modify `profiles.json`, its pinned digest, or any consumer's view of a
  profile's `status`, or cause any surface to report a profile as `ratified`;
- alter a multiplier, a threshold, a rated capacity, a computed trip point, an
  evaluator, the credible-reading rules, or the declared pre-command deadline;
- activate, evaluate, or actuate any pair other than the one it names, on this
  device or any other;
- lift, defer, or soften a hardened startup refusal, a binding verification
  stage, a provisional-binding restriction, or a posture requirement;
- register a dispatcher action, confer an action tier, or become reachable as
  one;
- reach the actuator by any path other than the zone's commissioned mapping and
  the runtime's ordinary actuation seam — there is no direct GPIO path, no
  test-only driver, and no bypass of the trip-intent journal, the durable trip
  record, or the evidence obligations;
- close a protected circuit as a consequence of expiry, session end, shutdown,
  or any other lifecycle event;
- confer anything on a skill. A skill-declared trigger on the same quantity is
  governed by the skills-package rules for its provenance, exactly as it is
  when no fixture is present, and a fixture neither revives a migrated Tier D
  declaration nor satisfies any check that a Tier D outcome's authority is the
  release-shipped registry.

**Trip state is not qualification state.** The trip-state machine, its latch,
its journal, its retries, and its local reset are exactly those of
[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#trip-state), unmodified. A trip
that occurs during a session is a real trip: it latches, it is durable, and it
is not undone when the window closes.

## Document Shape

A fixture covers one device and one pair.

```json
{
  "fixture": {
    "v": 1,
    "fixture_seq": 4,
    "device_id": "energy-monitor-bench-01",
    "issued_at_ms": 1800000000000,
    "not_after_ms": 1800604800000,
    "signer_id": "ori-release-qualification-2026",
    "signing_key": "ed25519:<base64-32-byte-public-key>",
    "binding_hash": "sha256:<64-lowercase-hex>",
    "binding_seq": 3,
    "zone_id": "main-distribution",
    "profile_id": "electrical.overcurrent.v1",
    "outcome": "open_protected_circuit",
    "max_sessions": 6,
    "session_limit_seconds": 1800,
    "actor": "…",
    "reason": "…"
  },
  "signature": "ed25519:<base64>"
}
```

| Field | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `v` | integer | Must be `1` |
| `fixture_seq` | integer | At least 1, strictly increasing per `device_id`. Distinct from `binding_seq` and from every other counter in the platform |
| `device_id` | string | Fleet alphabet. A fixture issued for one device MUST NOT be accepted by another |
| `issued_at_ms` | integer | Audit only. Never a freshness proof — `fixture_seq` is |
| `not_after_ms` | integer | Absolute expiry. Strictly greater than `issued_at_ms` |
| `signer_id` | string | Audit label only. Never an authority input |
| `signing_key` | string | The raw Ed25519 public key that signed this document, inside the signed body |
| `binding_hash` | string | Canonical hash of the binding this fixture is addressed to |
| `binding_seq` | integer | The `binding_seq` of that binding. Carried beside the hash so a mismatch is legible without recomputation, and checked against it |
| `zone_id` | string | Must name a zone of that binding |
| `profile_id` | string | Must name a profile of the shipped set whose `status` is `candidate` |
| `outcome` | string | Must equal the named profile's `outcome` |
| `max_sessions` | integer | At least 1. The total number of sessions this fixture may ever open on this device. Its ceiling is unresolved — see [Bounds](#bounds) |
| `session_limit_seconds` | integer | Positive. The maximum duration of one session. Its permitted range is unresolved — see [Bounds](#bounds) |
| `actor` | string | Who requested the fixture, per [firmware-mqtt-provisioning actor-and-reason](../firmware-mqtt-provisioning/v1.md#actor-and-reason) |
| `reason` | string | Why. Structurally required; absence is `malformed` |

**`outcome` is carried and checked, never used.** The outcome actuated is the
profile's, resolved through the zone's commissioned mapping at the moment of
actuation, exactly as an ordinary trip is. The field exists so that a fixture
whose issuer believed it was permitting something other than what the profile
commands is refused rather than executed, and it is deliberately redundant with
the profile set for the reason activation checks 6 and 7 are deliberately
redundant with the binding verifier: a check that holds only because an earlier
check held is not a boundary.

**There is no field by which a fixture can name a threshold, a multiplier, a
capacity, a sensor, an actuator, a pin, a coil state, a tier, or the profile's
pre-command deadline.** Their unrepresentability is the point. A fixture that
could carry one would be a channel through which an unratified parameter
reached a device under a signature, which is the authority failure this
estate's contracts are arranged to prevent.

**A fixture's own bounds are not that class of number.** `not_after_ms`,
`max_sessions` and `session_limit_seconds` bound how long and how often the
exercise may run. They are ceilings on exposure, they only ever narrow it, and
none of them is read by an evaluator, applied to a reading, or compared against
a latency. The deadline a fixture MUST NOT touch is the release-owned
pre-command intent-journal deadline, which the qualification record is written
to *measure* and which nothing in this document may select or alter.

### Bounds

`not_after_ms` MUST be strictly greater than `issued_at_ms`.

**The three exposure ceilings this contract imposes on an issuer — the maximum
validity window, the maximum `max_sessions`, and the permitted range of
`session_limit_seconds` — are unratified.** They are authority bounds, not
formatting limits: together they decide how much unratified Tier D authority
one signature can put on a device and for how long. They are chosen from the
qualification procedure and the bench schedule that procedure needs, not from a
document author's intuition.

The quantities each ceiling derives from, and the rehearsal that measures the
first of them, are in [procedure.md](procedure.md#deriving-the-three-exposure-ceilings).

**Until they are ratified, a conforming consumer MUST refuse every fixture,
with `limits_unratified`, before examining it.** This is the only safe reading
of an absent ceiling. A contract that named the fields, omitted their limits,
and left consumers to accept what arrived would authorise a signed
`max_sessions` of one million, or a `session_limit_seconds` spanning a week of
unattended autonomous evaluation, with no permitted grounds for refusal —
incomplete stated as permissive, which is the failure mode this estate refuses
everywhere else.

A consumer MUST NOT supply its own ceiling either. An implementation that
hardcodes a limit this contract has not decided has authored a safety bound in
a repository that does not own it, and the field-type table below carries these
constraints as unratified for that reason.

`limits_unratified` is a property of the contract and not of any document, so
it is decided before parsing, at stage 0 of the order below, over bytes nothing
has yet inspected. **It does not refuse a withdrawal**: a withdrawal only
removes authority, and a consumer must always be able to raise its floor.

Ratifying the ceilings is an amendment to this document that fills in three
numbers, deletes stage 0 and the `limits_unratified` verdict, and unblocks the
corpus. It is the first thing owed here.

### Retained State

A consumer retains, durably and per `device_id`:

| Retained | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `min_acceptable_seq` | The floor. No fixture with a lower `fixture_seq` is ever admissible again |
| `accepted_seq`, `accepted_hash` | The fixture currently held, or absent |
| `sessions_opened` | Sessions durably consumed under `accepted_hash` |

**Admission is by sequence and hash together.**

| Case | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| `fixture_seq` < `min_acceptable_seq` | `stale` |
| No fixture retained, `fixture_seq` >= `min_acceptable_seq` | Accepted as new; `min_acceptable_seq` := `fixture_seq`, `sessions_opened` := 0 |
| `fixture_seq` == `accepted_seq`, hash equal | Accepted. The same document, re-read; `sessions_opened` is preserved |
| `fixture_seq` == `accepted_seq`, hash different | `seq_reused` |
| `fixture_seq` > `accepted_seq` | Accepted; supersedes. Any open session closes first — see below — then `min_acceptable_seq` := `fixture_seq`, `sessions_opened` := 0 |

**A fixture is re-read, not re-issued.** Verification runs in full at every
startup and every session start, so an accepted fixture is verified many times
over its life, and each of those must reach the same verdict as the first. A
rule requiring the sequence to strictly exceed the retained mark would make a
fixture's own acceptance the reason it is refused at the next restart, and
`max_sessions` greater than one would hold only until the first reboot.

The hash is what makes the sequence safe to admit twice. Two different
documents claiming one sequence is a producer fault or a substitution attempt,
and `seq_reused` refuses both rather than accepting whichever arrived second.

**Supersession never happens underneath a live window.** A consumer accepting a
higher-sequence fixture while a session is open under the one it replaces MUST,
in this order: close the open session under the ordinary window-close rules,
recording `closed_by: superseded` — which command nothing, and which raise the
armed-close alert if the pair was armed — and only then replace the retained
fixture and reset `sessions_opened`.

Resetting the count first would leave a window running whose authorising
document is no longer held and whose session is no longer counted against
anything; the pair would be evaluating and able to actuate under a fixture the
consumer had already discarded. The same order applies to a withdrawal, which
[already states it](#withdrawal).

This case arises only for a consumer that re-reads the fixture file at a moment
of its own choosing. A consumer that reads at startup and session start alone
cannot reach it, because no session is open at either point. Neither behaviour
is required; the ordering is, for consumers that do the former.

### The Clock Is One Bound Of Three

`not_after_ms` is evaluated against the device clock, which this contract does
not authenticate and which a party with local access can move.

| Bound | Depends on the clock | Defeated by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `not_after_ms` | Yes | Moving the device clock backwards |
| `min_acceptable_seq` | No | Nothing available locally |
| `sessions_opened` against `max_sessions` | No | Nothing available locally |

`session_limit_seconds` is a fourth bound and is **not** on this list because it
is measured on a monotonic source, not on the device clock. That is a
requirement rather than an assumption; it is stated under *Measuring the
window*.

### Withdrawal

Withdrawal is a **separate signed document**, not a fixture that fails.

An issuer cannot withdraw by sending a fixture that refuses, because a refused
document changes nothing — that invariant is what makes every other refusal
safe, and a contract carrying one exception to it has the exception implemented
inconsistently by the second consumer. So withdrawal is expressed as a document
whose *acceptance* raises the floor, which is the ordinary rule rather than an
exception to it.

```json
{
  "withdrawal": {
    "v": 1,
    "device_id": "energy-monitor-bench-01",
    "issued_at_ms": 1800000000000,
    "withdraws_through": 4,
    "signer_id": "ori-release-qualification-2026",
    "signing_key": "ed25519:<base64-32-byte-public-key>",
    "actor": "…",
    "reason": "…"
  },
  "signature": "ed25519:<base64>"
}
```

It is delivered to `qualification/withdrawal.json`, beside the fixture, under
the same constant-path and no-other-route rules. It is verified through stages
1 to 5 of the order below — `parses`, `device_id`, `key_selection`,
`signature`, `authority` — and then one stage of its own:

| Stage | Refuses with |
| --- | --- |
| `withdrawal_freshness` | `stale`, when `withdraws_through` < `min_acceptable_seq` |

On acceptance a consumer MUST, in this order: set `min_acceptable_seq` to
`withdraws_through + 1` durably; discard any retained accepted fixture whose
`fixture_seq` falls at or below `withdraws_through`; and close any open session
belonging to it, under the window-close rules — which command nothing.

A withdrawal has no expiry and authorises nothing. It only ever removes, so
replaying an old one is a no-op refused as `stale`, and the sequence space it
raises is the fixture sequence space: after `withdraws_through: 4`, the next
usable fixture is `fixture_seq: 5`.

The floor, the accepted fixture's sequence and hash, and the sessions consumed
are reported on the health surface, so that a withdrawal is observable at the
device rather than inferred from a fixture that stopped working.

## Delivery And Verification

A fixture reaches the runtime as a file at
`qualification/fixture.json` under the installation's data directory — the
directory that holds `ori.yaml`, the state store, and `commissioning/`. The
path is a constant of this contract and of the installer, never a configuration
value, for the reason the binding's path is: a document that could name where
the next one is read from would choose its own successor.

**There is no other delivery route.** A fixture MUST NOT be accepted from a
gateway topic, a fleet API, a remote command, an MQTT payload, a skill package,
a DevicePolicy, an environment variable, a command-line argument, or any field
of any configuration document. A consumer that offers a second route has
created a remote path to Tier D authority over an unratified condition.

The runtime reads the file at startup and re-verifies it in full at every
session start. A file that fails any stage is not partially applied: no session
opens, no pair changes posture, and no retained state moves.

### Verification Order

There is exactly one order, and it is the `acceptance_order` the golden corpus
records. Fail closed at each stage.

| # | Stage | Refuses with |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | `contract_limits` | `limits_unratified` |
| 1 | `parses` | `malformed` |
| 2 | `device_id` | `wrong_device` |
| 3 | `key_selection` | `anchor_collision`, `unknown_signer` |
| 4 | `signature` | `bad_signature` |
| 5 | `authority` | `wrong_authority`, `superseded_signer` |
| 6 | `freshness` | `stale`, `seq_reused` |
| 7 | `validity` | `expired`, `window_too_long` |
| 8 | `posture` | `posture_forbidden` |
| 9 | `binding_match` | `binding_mismatch`, `binding_not_in_force` |
| 10 | `pair_resolution` | `unknown_profile`, `profile_not_candidate`, `unknown_zone`, `outcome_mismatch` |
| 11 | `pair_eligibility` | `not_eligible`, `unit_mismatch`, `direction_unsupported`, `parameter_mismatch`, `trip_point_unobservable`, `undemonstrated_binding`, `zone_contended` |

`anchor_collision` is evaluated at configuration load, before any fixture is
seen; it is listed at `key_selection` because that is the stage whose
precondition it is.

**Order is normative, not incidental.** A refusal is evidence that a consumer
performed a check only if every earlier check passed, so every reject vector
carries a `stage` naming where its verdict is decided, and a conforming
verifier must refuse it at that stage and no earlier one.

A withdrawal document runs stages 1 to 5 of this order and then
`withdrawal_freshness`, as [Withdrawal](#withdrawal) states. It never runs the
stages from `validity` onward: it authorises nothing, so it has nothing to be
eligible for.

**Stage 9 compares identity, not currency.** `binding_hash` MUST equal the
canonical hash of the binding **in force**, and `binding_seq` MUST equal that
binding's. A fixture addressed to a superseded binding is `binding_mismatch`,
not stale-but-usable: the zone it was reasoned about no longer exists in the
form the issuer saw. A binding that verified but is
[provisional](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md#provisional-and-in-force) is
`binding_not_in_force`.

**Stage 11 is the activation order, run early.** It is the same set of checks,
against the same zone facts, decided here so that an ineligible pair is refused
at acceptance rather than discovered at session start. It is run again at
activation, because it is a boundary and boundaries hold on their own.

It carries one check the activation order does not. **A fixture is refused with
`zone_contended` when any other profile is already active on the named zone.**
The trip-state machine is per pair and the actuator is not: two pairs deciding
one coil is a case this contract has not worked through, and v1 refuses it
rather than reasoning about it at the moment a coil moves. The refusal is
unreachable today, because no shipped profile is ratified and therefore no zone
carries an active profile; it is written now so that it is already in the
corpus when the first ratification makes it reachable.

### Posture

A consumer MUST refuse a fixture with `posture_forbidden` when
`device.deployment_profile` is `production`.

`development` and `staging` admit one. Staging is the posture a qualification
exercise wants: hardened startup, real refusals, and no production claim.

**This is defence in depth and not the control.** The deployment profile
arrives in a provisioning-signed document, so a site that mislabels itself is
not stopped by it. The controls are issuance and the device, binding, and pair
addressing; this refusal exists so that a fixture left on a device that is
later promoted stops working at promotion rather than quietly persisting.

## The Qualification Session

Acceptance permits nothing on its own. **A session is opened by a second,
independent act: a human at the installation.** Neither the artifact nor the
operator can open one alone, which is what makes a leaked or misdelivered
fixture inert on an unattended device.

A consumer offering the operation MUST constrain it to all of the following.

- **Local only** — reachable solely from an authenticated local session on the
  device, never from a gateway topic, a fleet API, a remote command, a
  dispatcher action, an action tier, or any configuration document.
- **Runtime-owned** — the operation is code the runtime owns. A local tool MAY
  invoke it and MUST NOT perform it: a tool that opens the window, evaluates
  readings, or drives the actuator itself produces evidence of what that tool
  did, which is not what a qualification record attests. The tool is transport
  and operator interface and nothing else.
- **Consented at the window, interactively** — consent is taken from the
  controlling terminal and MUST NOT be satisfiable by piped input, a flag, an
  environment variable, a cached or stored token, or any non-interactive path.
  Immediately before opening, the operation states the fixture hash, its
  expiry, the sessions remaining, the binding hash, the zone, the profile, the
  computed trip point and its unit, the outcome that will be commanded, the
  coil state that outcome resolves to under this zone's commissioned mapping,
  what losing coil power does to this zone, and that a window closing while the
  pair is armed leaves the protected circuit closed and unprotected. The
  consent is recorded in the qualification record.

  **A terminal does not prove presence.** Interactive consent establishes that
  an operator attested to opening the window, not that anyone is standing at
  the panel; an SSH session satisfies every technical condition above. Physical
  presence is a requirement of the qualification procedure, enforced by the
  people running it, and no consumer may report it as a property it verified.
- **Bounded** — the window closes at `session_limit_seconds` after opening, at
  `not_after_ms`, or when the operator closes it, whichever is first. It also
  closes at process loss, which is not a lesser case: see *Consuming a session
  durably*. How the elapsed duration is measured is normative — see *Measuring
  the window*.
- **Single-pair** — at most one session is open on a device at a time, and it
  covers only the fixture's pair.
- **Carrying no authority** — the session grants no tier, activates no other
  profile, and its success approves nothing else.

Consent is per window and not per trip. A trip fires on a reading, without an
operator, which is the behaviour under test; a consent prompt in that path
would measure something the ratified profile will never do. This differs
deliberately from the commissioning proof operation, whose commands are issued
by the operation itself and are therefore consentable individually.

### Session Refusals

| Refusal | When |
| --- | --- |
| `no_fixture` | No accepted fixture is held |
| `session_state_unreadable` | The durable session state cannot be read, or carries a malformed entry |
| `no_monotonic_clock` | No conforming monotonic time source is available |
| `sessions_exhausted` | `max_sessions` sessions have already been opened under this fixture hash |
| `session_open` | A session is already open on this device |
| `consent_declined` | The operator declined, or consent could not be taken interactively |
| `safety_path_unconfirmed` | The zone's actuator has not been confirmed drivable since the last start |
| `no_authority` | Attempted by a remote command, a skill, a configuration document, a DevicePolicy, or any non-local path |

A session refusal consumes nothing, and `session_state_unreadable` is the one
that says so least comfortably: a consumer that cannot read its own session
state MUST refuse to open a window rather than proceed on an unknown count. The
fail-closed direction here costs a qualification session and never authority.

### Measuring The Window

**`session_limit_seconds` is measured on a monotonic clock**: one that never
moves backwards and does not pause while the system is suspended. On Linux that
is `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` rather than `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, because a device suspended
mid-session would otherwise resume into a window whose remaining time had not
elapsed.

- A wall-clock adjustment — an NTP step, a manual set, a timezone or drift
  correction, in either direction — MUST NOT extend an open window. Elapsed
  time is what the monotonic source says it is.
- A wall-clock adjustment MAY close a window early, through `not_after_ms`,
  which is evaluated on wall time. Closing early is always safe; extending
  never is.
- The window closes at the **first** of: monotonic elapsed reaching
  `session_limit_seconds`, wall time reaching `not_after_ms`, operator close,
  an accepted withdrawal covering the fixture, a binding revision, a
  superseding fixture, or shutdown.
- A consumer that cannot read a conforming monotonic source MUST refuse to open
  a window, with `no_monotonic_clock`. A window whose duration cannot be
  measured is not bounded, whatever the fixture declares.

This is what makes the claim in *The Clock Is One Bound Of Three* true rather
than intended. Without it the session duration would be a fourth wall-clock
bound wearing the language of a durable one, and the local party who can move
the clock could hold a window open indefinitely.

### Consuming A Session Durably

`max_sessions` is a security bound, so it is enforced by a durable record
written before the window can do anything, in this order:

1. Take consent.
2. **Append a durable session-open record**, carrying the fixture hash, the
   pair, the `binding_seq` in force, the sequence this session occupies within
   `max_sessions`, and the open time.
3. Only then open the window: activate the pair, admit readings, permit arming.
4. On close, append a durable session-close record naming why it closed.

**The count advances at step 2 and never later.** A crash between consent and
the append leaves no session open and none consumed — nothing was authorised,
so nothing is spent. A crash after the append leaves the session **consumed**,
and a consumer MUST NOT reclaim it on the grounds that the window was short,
produced no trip, or never armed. Reclaiming a spent session is exactly the
mechanism by which `max_sessions` becomes unbounded: a party who can crash the
process between the increment and any later checkpoint would otherwise hold an
unlimited fixture.

The pattern is deliberately the trip-intent journal's, for the same reason and
with the same fail-closed reading rules: a session journal whose entries are
malformed, or which cannot be read at all, refuses to open a window.

**An open session never survives process loss.** There is no resumption. At
startup, a session-open record with no matching close is loaded as
`interrupted`, closed with that reason, and:

- the pair evaluates nothing and actuates nothing on account of it;
- the session stays consumed;
- a fresh interactive consent is required before any further window;
- if the pair was armed when the process died, the armed-close alert and health
  degradation below apply, because the protected circuit is closed and nothing
  is watching it;
- the qualification record for that session is written with what is known, and
  is marked interrupted rather than presented as a completed exercise.

This matters most on the platform the first sessions will run on. Process loss
on a Pi 4 was measured leaving the GPIO pad driving its last commanded level
with the line request freed (`ori-runtime` `docs/evidence/2026-09-01-pi4-gpio-controller-loss.md`),
so a restart after a session can find a coil still held in whatever state the
session left it. The restart path MUST NOT read an interrupted session as
licence to command that line in either direction: what the line does is
governed by the zone's conditioned startup rule in
[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#trip-state) and by whatever
supervisor coverage the deployment holds, unchanged and unaffected by this
contract.

### Inside The Window

The pair activates and evaluates exactly as a ratified pair would. Arming,
credible-reading rules, verdicts, the bounded trip-intent append, the outcome
command through the commissioned mapping, the durable record, the retry loop,
and local reset are all
[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#trip-state) behaviour,
unmodified. Arming is available and is how the protected circuit is closed
before a condition is induced; its ordinary refusals apply.

Every durable artifact a session writes — the intent entry, the trip record,
the evidence rows — MUST carry the fixture hash, so that an audit can never
mistake a qualification trip for a protection trip. A consumer that writes a
qualification trip indistinguishable from an ordinary one has produced a record
that will later be read as a protection claim.

### When The Window Closes

On close, for any reason, the pair returns to `pending_ratification` and stops
evaluating.

**The close commands nothing, in either direction.** A pair that tripped stays
tripped, latched, and durable. A pair that was armed leaves the protected
circuit closed, because arming closed it and no lifecycle event may actuate.
Restoring the panel to its intended state is the operator's step in the
procedure, and it is stated in the record. **Its order matters:** reset is
available only while the window is open, so a tripped pair is reset before the
window closes or not at all. Once it has closed, restoration is through
controls outside the runtime.

**A window closing while the pair is armed MUST raise a Tier A alert and
degrade health**, naming the zone and the closed circuit. That state — a
protected circuit the runtime itself closed, with no authority left to evaluate
the condition that was watching it — is the one outcome of this operation that
is worse than not having run it, and it is reachable by simply walking away
from an open session. It is announced rather than merely recorded, and it
persists until the operator opens the circuit or a new authority covers it.

**The retry obligation survives the close.** A pair left `tripped` with a
command status other than `command_issued` keeps attempting its outcome on the
bounded-backoff schedule
[safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#trip-state) defines, from a loop
that does not depend on sensor delivery, until the driver accepts, a local
reset succeeds, or the consumer shuts down. Losing the authority to evaluate a
condition is not licence to stop opening a circuit that was already decided
open.

A window also closes immediately, and with the same rules, when the binding in
force is revised, when the fixture expires, when a withdrawal covering it is
accepted, when a higher-sequence fixture supersedes it, at shutdown, and — as
`interrupted`, discovered at the next start — on process loss. A pair whose zone
leaves the binding while tripped is an
[orphan](../safety-profile/v1.md#trip-state) and follows that contract's
orphan-retirement path; a fixture confers no exception to it.

**A tripped pair whose window has closed cannot be reset, and nothing about
that close actuates.** The pair stays `tripped`, latched, with the protected
circuit as the trip left it, and an attempted reset is refused with
`no_evaluation_authority`.

Reset is licensed by the most recent credible reading not satisfying the
condition, and evaluating a reading is exactly what the closed window withdrew.
A reset that closed the circuit regardless would be an unconditioned close
command issued by a pair that had stopped checking — a worse outcome than a
circuit left open, which is what the trip commanded and what the operator
expects to find.

The exits are a fresh session, after which ordinary reset governs, or the
operator restoring the panel through controls outside the runtime. This is the
one place where the trip-state machine as written in the safety profile
contract does not cover the case, and the amendment is named under
[Amendments Required In Neighbouring Contracts](#amendments-required-in-neighbouring-contracts).

## Posture And Reporting

A pair's qualification state is a closed vocabulary, reported per pair on the
health surface:

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | No accepted fixture names this pair |
| `fixture_accepted` | A fixture is accepted; no session is open |
| `session_open` | A window is open and the pair is evaluating |
| `retired` | A fixture that was accepted this run has expired, been superseded, or been exhausted |

**A pair whose qualification state is anything but `none` MUST be reported as
`supervised qualification; protection claim unavailable`, on every
operator-visible surface, in every posture.** A consumer MUST NOT report such a
pair as protected, MUST NOT report its profile as active or ratified, and MUST
NOT include it in any count, badge, summary, or aggregate that a reader would
take as protection coverage. The wording matters because the failure it
prevents is a demonstration video, a screenshot, or a dashboard tile being read
as a protection claim six months later.

Alongside the state, the surface carries the fixture hash, its expiry, sessions
used and remaining, `min_acceptable_seq`, and the pair's ordinary trip state
and command status. Command status remains
[evidence-bounded](../safety-profile/v1.md#trip-state): "trip command issued;
physical outcome unverified" until something verified the effect, and a
qualification session does not change what the runtime knows about the circuit.

The health payload has no field for any of this today. The vocabulary belongs
in [runtime-health/v2.md](../runtime-health/v2.md) and the runtime PR
implementing a consumer MUST NOT invent it — the same discipline
[ori-specs#161](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-specs/issues/161) applies to
supervision posture.

## The Qualification Record

A session produces one record, locally, whether or not it produced a trip. A
session that induced no trip is evidence too, and a procedure that only records
successes cannot support a ratification argument.

| Group | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | `v`, `record_id`, `device_id`, `fixture_hash`, `fixture_seq`, `binding_hash`, `binding_seq`, `zone_id`, `profile_id` |
| Condition | The condition kind, its release-owned parameters as evaluated, the zone's `rated_capacity` where the kind uses one, and the computed trip point with its unit |
| Session | `opened_at_ms`, `closed_at_ms`, the monotonic elapsed duration, `closed_by` (`operator`, `session_limit`, `expiry`, `withdrawal`, `superseded`, `binding_revision`, `shutdown`, `interrupted`), the session's sequence within `max_sessions`, the consent record, and the operator identity |
| Supervision | The supervision posture at open — `supervised`, `unsupervised`, or `unknown` |
| Observation | The count of readings evaluated, their verdicts by class, and the readings within a declared window either side of each trip |
| Trip | For each trip: the reading, the verdict, `intent_append_ms`, `command_issue_ms`, the command status, the effect-verification result, and the `binding_seq` in force |
| Outcome | What the operator did to restore the circuit, and the pair's final trip state |
| Claim | `claim`, a closed vocabulary whose only v1 value is `supervised_qualification_evidence` |

**The latencies are the point of recording them.** `intent_append_ms` is
measured from the decision to trip to the trip-intent append completing or its
deadline expiring, and `command_issue_ms` from that decision to the driver
reporting acceptance or refusal. `electrical.overcurrent.v1` cannot be ratified
without demonstrating that the declared pre-command deadline is tolerable for
its hazard, and a record without measured numbers cannot support that.

**Supervision is recorded, not required.** A consumer holding the release-owned
process supervisor
([ori-runtime#484](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-runtime/issues/484))
records its posture; one that does not records `unsupervised`. It is not a gate
because the supervisor does not exist yet and gating on an unbuilt mechanism
would block the evidence this contract exists to produce — but a record that
omitted it would let a ratification review assume coverage that was never
present.

**A record is an input to a ratification review and is not itself an argument.**
It carries no verdict field, no pass/fail, and no recommendation. What a review
must establish for a given profile belongs to
[safety-profile/ratification.md](../safety-profile/ratification.md), which is
not this contract.

Where the record is delivered, how it is sealed, and how a review consumes it
are not defined here; the local record is the obligation, and its carriage is
recorded under Open Questions.

## Canonical Bytes

Signatures are over the canonical serialisation of the `fixture` object using
the canonical form of
[gateway-mqtt-canonical-json/v1.md](../gateway-mqtt-canonical-json/v1.md):
recursively sorted keys, no insignificant whitespace, RFC 8259 escaping,
caller-ordered arrays. Numbers are additionally constrained to the D-011
agreement zone of [evidence/v2.md](../evidence/v2.md).

The rules that only bytes can carry are inherited unchanged from
[commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md#canonical-bytes):
a number's spelling is fixed by its field's declared type and any other
spelling is `malformed` at `parses`; an object MUST NOT carry the same key
twice at any level and a verifier reading bytes MUST refuse a duplicate during
parsing; unpaired surrogates are refused. They are inherited rather than
restated so the two contracts cannot drift.

### The Closed Grammar

Every object admits exactly its declared keys. An unrecognised key is
`malformed`, never ignored.

| Object | Exactly these keys |
| --- | --- |
| fixture | `v`, `fixture_seq`, `device_id`, `issued_at_ms`, `not_after_ms`, `signer_id`, `signing_key`, `binding_hash`, `binding_seq`, `zone_id`, `profile_id`, `outcome`, `max_sessions`, `session_limit_seconds`, `actor`, `reason` |
| withdrawal | `v`, `device_id`, `issued_at_ms`, `withdraws_through`, `signer_id`, `signing_key`, `actor`, `reason` |
| fixture envelope | `fixture`, `signature` |
| withdrawal envelope | `withdrawal`, `signature` |

**Both envelope forms are closed and both are exercised.** A corpus covering
only the fixture cannot tell a conforming implementation from one that
validates fixtures strictly and withdrawals loosely — and a loosely validated
withdrawal is a document that removes authority, which is the half an attacker
has least reason to forge and a defective consumer has most reason to
mishandle.

The envelope is closed for the reason the binding's is: a field beside the
signature sits outside the signed bytes and is unauthenticated by construction.

### Field Types

| Field | Type | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `v` | integer | exactly `1` |
| `fixture_seq`, `binding_seq`, `withdraws_through` | integer | at least 1; not a float, not a boolean |
| `issued_at_ms`, `not_after_ms` | integer | positive; not a float, not a string |
| `max_sessions` | integer | at least 1; ceiling unresolved |
| `session_limit_seconds` | integer | positive; range unresolved |
| `device_id`, `signer_id`, `actor`, `reason`, `zone_id` | string | non-empty after trimming whitespace |
| `profile_id` | string | the profile id grammar of [safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#profile-shape) |
| `outcome` | string | exactly `open_protected_circuit` in v1 |
| `signing_key` | string | `ed25519:` + canonical base64 of 32 bytes |
| `signature` | string | `ed25519:` + canonical base64 of 64 bytes |
| `binding_hash` | string | `sha256:` + 64 lowercase hex |

A boolean is not a number and a number is not a boolean; an implementation MUST
exclude booleans wherever an integer is required. Empty and whitespace-only
strings are `malformed` wherever a string is required.

## Error Semantics

Acceptance verdicts, each decided at the stage the corpus records and at no
earlier one:

| Verdict | Decided at | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `limits_unratified` | contract_limits | This contract's exposure ceilings are not yet ratified; every fixture is refused, no withdrawal is |
| `malformed` | parses | Shape, grammar, unknown key, missing required field, value outside its constraint, number outside the agreement zone, number spelled other than its field's declared type requires, duplicate object key, unpaired surrogate |
| `wrong_device` | device_id | Addressed to a different device |
| `anchor_collision` | key_selection | A qualification anchor equals the provisioning, commissioning, or config-signing anchor as raw bytes; evaluated at configuration load |
| `unknown_signer` | key_selection | `signing_key` matches no configured qualification anchor, or no current anchor is configured |
| `bad_signature` | signature | Signature does not verify against `signing_key` |
| `wrong_authority` | authority | The verified signer is an anchor of another purpose |
| `superseded_signer` | authority | The verified signer is the previous qualification generation |
| `stale` | freshness, withdrawal_freshness | A fixture's `fixture_seq` is below `min_acceptable_seq`, or a withdrawal's `withdraws_through` is below it |
| `seq_reused` | freshness | A different document claims the `fixture_seq` already accepted, by hash |
| `expired` | validity | `not_after_ms` has passed |
| `window_too_long` | validity | The validity window exceeds this contract's maximum. The verdict is defined; the maximum is [unresolved](#bounds) |
| `posture_forbidden` | posture | `device.deployment_profile` is `production` |
| `binding_mismatch` | binding_match | `binding_hash` or `binding_seq` is not that of the binding in force |
| `binding_not_in_force` | binding_match | The named binding verified but is provisional |
| `unknown_profile` | pair_resolution | `profile_id` names no profile of the shipped set |
| `profile_not_candidate` | pair_resolution | The named profile's `status` is not `candidate` |
| `unknown_zone` | pair_resolution | `zone_id` names no zone of the binding in force |
| `outcome_mismatch` | pair_resolution | `outcome` differs from the named profile's outcome |
| `not_eligible` | pair_eligibility | The zone's sensor quantity does not match the profile's `observes.quantity` |
| `unit_mismatch` | pair_eligibility | As [safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md#error-semantics) |
| `direction_unsupported` | pair_eligibility | As safety-profile/v1 |
| `parameter_mismatch` | pair_eligibility | As safety-profile/v1 |
| `trip_point_unobservable` | pair_eligibility | As safety-profile/v1 |
| `undemonstrated_binding` | pair_eligibility | As safety-profile/v1 |
| `zone_contended` | pair_eligibility | Another profile is already active on the named zone |

Session refusals are decided at session start, over an already accepted
fixture, and are listed under [Session Refusals](#session-refusals):
`no_fixture`, `session_state_unreadable`, `sessions_exhausted`, `session_open`,
`consent_declined`, `safety_path_unconfirmed`, `no_authority`.

**A refusal never disturbs what is already held.** A refused fixture leaves the
floor, the accepted fixture, the session counts, every pair's posture, and
every trip state exactly as they were, and commands nothing. Withdrawal is not
an exception to this: it moves state by being **accepted**, not by failing.

- Refuse every fixture with `limits_unratified` while this contract's exposure
  ceilings are unratified, and never substitute a locally chosen ceiling.

- Write a qualification record for every session, including sessions that

masked the refusal under test in order to exercise the cases behind it would be
measuring the mask, and would report coverage no consumer has.

**What the reference procedure does and does not establish.** It decides
withdrawal verdicts from bytes, so a case that declares the wrong verdict, the
wrong stage, or the wrong state transition is refused rather than believed —
which is what makes those cases coverage of *this contract's rules*. It is not
evidence about any consumer: no implementation exists, and the checker's
agreement with the corpus says nothing about a runtime's. It also decides
nothing about a fixture, because stage 0 refuses every fixture first.

Ratification of this corpus still requires an independent verifier in a
language other than this one, as the neighbouring corpora do. The generator
lives in the first implementing repository, not here, and shares no code with
this checker.

## Open Questions

- **Qualification key custody.** Issuance, storage, rotation, and revocation of
  the qualification key are not settled. It is a Tier D authority key over
  unratified conditions and it is held by the release owner; where it lives,
  who may operate it, and what a compromise recovery looks like are unanswered,
  and this contract does not pretend otherwise. It shares this gap with the
  commissioning key, and the two should probably be answered together.
- **The three exposure ceilings, which block everything else here.** The
  maximum validity window, the maximum `max_sessions`, and the permitted range
  of `session_limit_seconds` are undecided, so stage 0 refuses every fixture and
  no consumer may implement this contract. They bound how much unratified Tier
  D authority one signature places on a device, so they are chosen from the
  qualification procedure and the bench schedule that procedure needs — not
  from a plausible-looking number. Settling them is the amendment that deletes
  stage 0, converts the deferred fixture cases into exercised ones, and
  unblocks the end-to-end corpus and the first consumer.
- **Revocation without reaching the device.** A withdrawal is a file delivered
  to the device's data directory. A device that cannot be reached holds its
  fixture until expiry, so the validity ceiling is also the worst-case
  revocation delay — which is one of the inputs to choosing it. Whether an
  online freshness check or a denial list is warranted is unsettled; an online
  check would make a safety instrument depend on connectivity, which is the
  coupling the estate refuses everywhere else.
- **Carriage of the qualification record.** The record is written locally. How
  it is sealed, delivered, and consumed by a ratification review is not defined
  here and belongs with the evidence exchange work.
- **What a review does with a record.** This contract produces evidence and
  defines no threshold for sufficiency. How many sessions, on how many
  circuits, at what capacities, before a profile's ratification argument is
  complete is decided per profile in its dossier, under
  [safety-profile/ratification.md](../safety-profile/ratification.md). A
  single fan-and-clamp trip proves one commissioned setup behaved as specified;
  it does not establish that a multiplier is safe for every future circuit, and
  no number of bench sessions on one bench will.
- **`firmware_channel` zones.** A fixture is expressible against one, but the
  control leg of such a zone
  [cannot yet be proven](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md#a-mapping-has-two-proof-legs-and-one-method-proves-one-leg),
  so such a zone is never in force and the case is unreachable in practice. It
  becomes reachable when [ori-specs#159](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-specs/issues/159)
  lands, and this contract should be re-read against it then.
- **Interaction with a ratified profile on the same zone.** v1 refuses it, with
  `zone_contended`, because two pairs commanding one coil is a case neither
  this contract nor the safety profile contract has worked through. The refusal
  is unreachable while no profile is ratified, and it becomes a real
  restriction the moment one is — a device could then be unable to qualify a
  second condition on a zone it already protects. Working the case through, or
  accepting the restriction deliberately, is owed before the first
  ratification.
