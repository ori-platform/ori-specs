# Customer Alerts Contract v1

> Status: maturity `draft`; compatibility `amendable`; adoption: none
> recorded; proof: end-to-end `none`, HIL `none`, production `none`. Record:
> [status/customer-alerts/v1.json](../status/customer-alerts/v1.json).
>
> **This version is not yet implementable for all four classes.** The
> measurement, calibration and timing decisions under [Open bounds](#open-bounds)
> are unresolved. A shipped customer switch is not evidence that the runtime
> detects its named condition. See [#393](https://github.com/ori-platform/ori-specs/issues/393).

## Purpose and boundaries

This contract defines the meaning of four customer-selected optional alert
classes. It does not grant authority to change a supply, open a circuit, or
decide a Tier D safety condition. A preference can withhold an optional
external notice. It cannot withhold the physical act, its audit record, a
mandatory protection-degraded notice, or a Tier D notification.

`device-policy/v1` is frozen by shipment. Its base fields and verification
rules remain unchanged. The `alerts` object below is an optional **extension**
to a verified DevicePolicy for consumers implementing this contract. The
extension neither redefines v1's base fields nor permits an unverified object
to govern a notice. A consumer that does not implement this extension ignores
it; a product must not show a toggle as effective for that consumer.

## Signed preference extension

The optional `alerts` member is an object whose known members are booleans:

| Member | Customer meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `generator_running_too_long` | Generator continues running for at least two hours after grid restoration |
| `unusual_consumption_spike` | Consumption is significantly above a valid site baseline |
| `battery_underperforming` | Battery output is below a justified expectation given measured charge input |
| `grid_power_restored` | Grid input has returned after a confirmed outage |

The product signs the **entire** policy including the extension under the
existing DevicePolicy signing protocol. An unverified `alerts` object is never
applied. An absent `alerts` member, an absent known member, or a member whose
value is not a JSON boolean cannot suppress that class. Unknown member names
cannot suppress any known class. Only an explicit JSON `false` for a known
class in a verified, current policy can withhold that class's optional notice.
An expired or refused policy cannot introduce a new suppression. The
underlying policy rejection and retained-policy behavior remains that of
`device-policy/v1` and its implemented fetch path; this extension does not
authorize replacing a previously verified policy with unauthenticated data.

The product MUST NOT expose `protection_degraded` as a toggle or signed
`alerts` key. The runtime MUST NOT consult `alerts` to decide whether to
attempt a Tier D act or its safety notification. A mandatory notice's
availability is an operational condition, not a customer's preference.

## Evidence before detection

An alert class is a claim about a site, not a label attached to an arbitrary
skill trigger. A positive claim requires a named first-party detector whose
inputs are commissioned for that site, fresh, quality-qualified and coherent
over the required interval. Missing, stale, invalid or contradictory evidence
MUST NOT count as the positive condition. It is **unavailable/uncertain**, not
"normal" and not "disabled by customer". The product must be able to convey
that distinction when it presents a switch or an alert history.

The runtime MUST bind a disableable class to a reviewed first-party skill
identity **and** trigger identity. A community skill using the same trigger
name MUST NOT inherit the binding. Notification suppression MUST occur only
after the detector has established a positive condition and before an
optional provider call. It MUST NOT suppress a separate audit or action path.

### `generator_running_too_long`

A positive claim requires an independently observed grid-restoration event
and a confirmed generator-running signal that remains valid for the next
**two hours**. The timer begins no earlier than the restoration event. A
source-transfer request or a load measurement is not proof that the generator
is running. An observed stop, an outage recurrence, or loss of trusted
generator state invalidates the continuous interval. A runtime restart MUST
NOT infer two hours from a persisted timestamp alone; the state and its
continuity must be re-established or the timer must restart.

### `unusual_consumption_spike`

A positive claim requires a measured site consumption quantity, a valid
baseline for the **same quantity and site**, sufficient fresh samples, a
defined exceedance criterion, and exclusion of known expected context. A
single current reading is not automatically site power or energy use. The
existing first-party `energy-anomaly-detector/sudden_load_spike` is a candidate
binding, not proof that every site has the necessary calibrated measurement.

### `battery_underperforming`

A positive claim requires measured charge energy entering the battery and
measured later usable output over a comparable, complete interval or cycle,
plus a commissioned expected-performance model and tolerance for that battery
and its operating conditions. Low state of charge, absence of charging in
strong sun, or low PV output alone cannot satisfy this class. If the intended
customer meaning is instead "not charging despite solar input", the product
wording and class definition must be changed through review before binding an
existing trigger. The Tier D battery cutoff is never a customer-toggle target.

### `grid_power_restored`

A positive claim requires a documented grid-input sensor to have observed a
confirmed unavailable state followed by a stable available state. The alert
is about **grid input**, whether or not the load transferred to it. A lone
above-threshold voltage sample, a generator-load assumption, or a requested
Tier B source transfer is not a restoration event. A restoration notice is
informational; suppressing it cannot suppress recording an actual physical
source change or its result.

## Delivery outcomes

The runtime owns provider sends and durable local retry. Product reporting
describes observed attempts; it is never an authority dependency for physical
protection. `alert_id` identifies the logical alert. A
`delivery_attempt_id` identifies one provider attempt for one channel. A
retransmitted report after a lost API acknowledgement MUST reuse that attempt
ID without calling the provider again. A new provider call MUST mint a new
attempt ID. SMS and WhatsApp outcomes are independent.

`accepted` means the provider accepted the request. It is not a handset
receipt. `failed`, `capped` and `disabled` have distinct meanings; the last
two do not imply a provider call. `delivered` MUST NOT be asserted without a
separate receipt protocol that proves the delivery scope being claimed. Push
or local Android notification permission is a different channel and cannot
serve as SMS or WhatsApp delivery proof.

## Open bounds

Before v1 can be treated as implementable, the contract and vectors must
settle the following. Until then the three unmatched classes remain
unavailable in the product, rather than silently bound to an inexact trigger.

1. A commissioned, first-party source for generator-running state and for
   grid-input availability on supported deployment types. Simulated
   generator power is a test input, not field proof. Specify signal freshness,
   debounce, gap tolerance and persistence across restart.
2. The physical quantity, baseline period, minimum sample evidence,
   exceedance criterion and context exclusions for consumption spikes.
3. The battery input/output accounting window, expected-performance model,
   calibration source, minimum data completeness and environmental tolerance.
   Decide with Product whether the existing UI wording is supportable.
4. The grid outage/restoration voltage or discrete-state criteria, stable
   dwell, and reset/re-arm semantics. Availability must not be inferred from
   a source-transfer command.
5. Cooldown/repeat semantics for each class, including whether a continuing
   condition can generate a new logical alert after a provider failure.
6. Positive and refusal vectors for every class, policy absence/unknown/
   invalid values, missing sensor evidence, restart and safety independence;
   generator and battery field proof where the claim depends on hardware.
7. The exact signed-policy canonicalization and compatibility check for
   shipped product and runtime consumers. The extension cannot change the
   frozen DevicePolicy v1 baseline by implication.

No end-to-end, HIL or production provider proof is claimed by this draft.
