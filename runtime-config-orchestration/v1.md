# Runtime Configuration Orchestration v1

> Status: Design target, pre-ratification. No shipped consumer claims
> conformance. Amendable in place under the
> [pre-ratification exception](../VERSIONING.md#pre-ratification-exception).

A device's configuration is not one document. It is a **set** of artifacts,
each signed by a different authority, each verified under its own contract,
and each meaning nothing on its own about whether the device may act. This
contract says which artifacts make up the set, which anchor verifies each,
how they compose into one accepted configuration, what a consumer does when
one verifies and another does not, and how a device provisioned under the
single-document format crosses over and never crosses back.

It wraps nothing. Every artifact keeps its own envelope, its own signature,
and its own verification order; this contract adds no bytes to any of them
and carries no byte vectors of its own. What it owns is the relationships.

## Why A Split Needs An Orchestrator

Splitting the configuration by signing authority is what makes a field in the
wrong document a refusal rather than a convention. It also creates the seam
this contract exists to close: artifacts that are each valid, each signed by
the right key, and jointly wrong. A new hardware inventory beside an old
commissioned binding is the concrete case — every signature verifies, and the
binding describes contactors the device no longer claims to have. Applying
the artifacts independently composes a device out of two incompatible halves.

## The Set

| Artifact | Contract | Verified against | Presence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Provisioning document | [runtime-config/v2.md](../runtime-config/v2.md) | the **provisioning** anchor | Required. Nothing runs without it |
| Entitlement document | [device-policy/v1.md](../device-policy/v1.md) | the **commercial** anchor | Optional. Absent means no paid capability, and nothing else |
| Commissioned safety binding | [commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md](../commissioned-safety-binding/v1.md) | the **commissioning** anchor, current or previous | Required when the provisioning document declares actuating hardware; otherwise absent |
| Safety profiles | [safety-profile/v1.md](../safety-profile/v1.md) | nothing — shipped and signed with the release | Not a configuration artifact. Listed so that no document can be mistaken for its carrier |

**Three anchors, not four.** The release is an authority boundary and not a
configuration signer: safety profiles ship inside the signed runtime release,
so there is no document to verify and no anchor to configure. A consumer that
accepts a "profile document" from any anchor is non-conforming.

**Actuating hardware** means a provisioning document with
`actions.relay.gpio_pin` present, or any `firmware_channel` actuator in the
inventory. The declaration is inventory — that the hardware exists. Which
sensor it protects, what its coil states do, and what the circuit is rated for
are the binding's, and arrive under a different key.

## Anchors

An anchor is a raw Ed25519 public key, delivered **out of band** — by the
installer into a location this contract fixes, never inside any artifact of
the set — and labelled with the class and generation it holds.

| Anchor | Class | Generation | Fixed location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `provisioning_current` | provisioning | may author | `ORI_CONFIG_TRUST_ANCHOR_PUBLIC_KEY_B64` |
| `provisioning_previous` | provisioning | verify-only | `ORI_CONFIG_TRUST_ANCHOR_PREVIOUS_PUBLIC_KEY_B64`, per the design-target section of [runtime-config-signing/v1.md](../runtime-config-signing/v1.md) |
| `commercial` | entitlement | may author | `ORI_DEVICE_POLICY_PUBLIC_KEY_B64` |
| `commissioning_current` | commissioning | may author | `ORI_COMMISSIONING_ANCHOR_PUBLIC_KEY_B64`, per the binding contract |
| `commissioning_previous` | commissioning | verify-only | `ORI_COMMISSIONING_ANCHOR_PREVIOUS_PUBLIC_KEY_B64`, per the binding contract |

**No artifact of the set selects an anchor, by key or by name.** A document
that could name the variable its verifier is read from would choose that
verifier by indirection, and `runtime-config/v2` retires every such selector
as `forbidden_content`. The locations above are constants of the contract,
owned by the release and the installer. A host that must relocate the
provisioning anchor does so with the host-level `ORI_CONFIG_TRUST_ANCHOR_ENV`
override, which no document carries.

**`anchor_collision` is decided at configuration load, before any artifact
is seen.** If any two configured anchors are equal as raw bytes — across
classes or across generations — the separation this contract rests on does
not exist, and no document can repair it. The consumer refuses to start in
every posture. The binding contract states this rule for its own two anchors
against the provisioning anchor; this contract extends it to the whole set.

A previous-generation anchor is **verify-only**: it verifies artifacts that
were accepted under it and may not author new ones, or rotating a compromised
key achieves nothing. Artifacts already accepted stay accepted after rotation;
they are not re-verified, and re-signing a proven binding to change its key
would demand a site visit for a cryptographic event.

## Selection, Then Signature, Then Authority

The provisioning document and the binding both carry `signing_key` inside
their signed bytes. For each, a consumer proceeds in exactly this order:

1. **`key_selection`.** Compare `signing_key` as raw bytes against **every
   configured anchor of every class and generation**. Exactly one may match.
   None is `unknown_signer`. No verdict about authority is issued here, and no
   signature is attempted against a key the document did not name.
2. **`signature`.** Verify against the single selected key. Failure is
   `bad_signature`.
3. **`authority`.** Only now, over a document proven to be signed by the key it
   names: a selected anchor of another class is `wrong_authority`; the
   previous generation of the right class is `superseded_signer`.

**Authority is decided over a verified document, never before.** If
`wrong_authority` were decided at selection, anyone could name a public key
they do not hold — a public key is not a secret — over arbitrary bytes and
manufacture a `wrong_authority` event on any device they can reach. Deciding
it after verification means only the holder of that key can cause its own
verdict.

**And selection is anchor-wide, or the verdict is dead text.** A consumer that
selects the anchor by the document's *class* — "this is a provisioning
document, so try the provisioning key" — can never reach `wrong_authority`: a
provisioning document signed by the commissioning key fails the signature and
is reported as `bad_signature`, sending an operator to key custody when the
finding is a signer writing outside its authority. That is why v2 added
`signing_key` to the provisioning envelope.

This is still not trial verification. One key, chosen by comparing key
material; one signature check; never a second attempt on failure.

**The entitlement document is single-anchor.** DevicePolicy carries no
`signing_key` and is verified against the commercial anchor only; a failure
there is `bad_signature` and `wrong_authority` is unreachable for it. That is
deliberate: DevicePolicy is an implemented baseline, and widening it is a
separate versioning decision. The consequence is bounded because the
entitlement document can carry no safety authority in any case.

## What A Document May Say

Content authority is decided **after** signature and authority, over a
document whose signer is established:

| Verdict | Decided by | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `malformed` | the artifact's own contract | Belongs to nobody |
| `foreign_field` | the artifact's own contract | Belongs to another authority; validly signed and still refused |
| `forbidden_content` | the artifact's own contract | Belongs to no configuration authority — an anchor, or release-owned |

The three are distinct because they send an operator to three different
places, and none of them is a signature failure. `runtime-config/v2` fixes
which retired keys carry which verdict for the provisioning document; the
binding contract's closed grammar does the same for the binding; DevicePolicy
carries no field that can affect Tier A or Tier D behaviour, which is the
property `ori-cloud` #19 tests for.

No artifact of the set may carry an action-tier declaration, a Tier D
condition, a safety-profile parameter, a safety multiplier, or a capability
grant. Ordinary Tier A through C tuning is permitted where a skill's schema
allows it; the prohibition is on safety authority, not on numbers.

## Freshness

**The provisioning document is ordered by `device.inventory_generation`.**
The generation is monotonic per device and never reused for different
contents — both guaranteed by v2's producer rules — so a consumer holds:

| Incoming generation vs accepted | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| greater | accepted; the inventory advances |
| equal, identical canonical bytes | accepted; idempotent re-application, no state change |
| equal, different content | `generation_reused` — a producer violated its own rule, and the document is refused |
| lower | `stale` — a replayed older document |

`signed_at_ms` is audit only and is never a freshness proof, because a clock
is not a counter.

**The binding is ordered by `binding_seq`**, under its own contract's
`freshness` stage, with one addition: a binding held pending counts. A
binding whose `binding_seq` does not exceed both the accepted and the pending
binding's is `stale`, so a pending binding can only ever be replaced by a
fresher one.

## Composition: The Inventory Invariant

Actuation is licensed by one equality, and by nothing weaker:

```text
accepted provisioning document . device.inventory_generation
    == accepted commissioned binding . inventory_generation
```

Holding a provisioning document and a binding that name different generations
is not a usable configuration set. The consumer never resolves it by keeping
part of one: **no intermediate state grants actuation authority.**

| Situation | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Provisioning advances; the accepted binding is now **behind** | The provisioning document applies. Actuation is withdrawn for every zone: actuating profiles go inactive and report `inventory_mismatch`; observation, alerting and Tier C proposals continue. The binding is retained for audit |
| A binding arrives **ahead** of the accepted provisioning generation | Not applied. It is retained as pending, replaced only by a fresher pending binding, and applied in the same step as the provisioning document that reaches its generation |
| A binding arrives **behind** the accepted provisioning generation | Not applied. It describes hardware the device no longer declares |
| A binding arrives **at** the accepted generation and passes its own verification | Applied; the invariant holds; actuation is enabled where the binding's zones and the release profiles permit |

The site-visit ordering this produces is the correct one. Rewiring a
contactor is a site visit already; the provisioning inventory advances to
describe the new hardware, protection is withdrawn until someone proves the
new arrangement, and the proof re-enables it. Neither order of arrival lets a
proven mapping for old hardware drive new hardware.

## Application And Partial Failure

Each artifact **applies whole or not at all**, and a refused artifact leaves
the previously accepted artifact of its class in force — not a merge, not a
partial overlay. The set as a whole then re-evaluates the invariant.

At **startup**, with the accepted set loaded from durable state:

| Condition | Hardened posture | Development posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No accepted provisioning document | refuse | refuse |
| Actuating hardware declared, no accepted binding | refuse | start degraded; no protection claim |
| Actuating hardware declared, binding behind the inventory | refuse | start degraded; no protection claim |
| No entitlement document, or an expired one | start; no paid capability | start; no paid capability |
| `anchor_collision` | refuse | refuse |

Hardened posture is `staging`, `production`, or `development` with
`security.enforce_production_posture`, as in
[runtime-config/v1.md](../runtime-config/v1.md). A device that starts
unprotected and reports healthy is the worst available outcome, and the two
refusals for a missing or behind binding are the same rule as the binding
contract's `unbound_actuator` seen from the set.

At **runtime**, an artifact arriving while the consumer is running never
stops it. A refused artifact is reported and the accepted set stands; a
provisioning advance that leaves the binding behind withdraws actuation and
degrades health rather than crashing a running device. The startup rules
apply again at the next start.

**Entitlement is retained, never discarded.** A fetch that fails, or a
document that is refused, leaves the last verified entitlement in force;
revocation is carried by `valid_until`, exactly as
[device-policy/v1.md](../device-policy/v1.md) already requires. Discarding
would switch paid capability off for a customer whose network dropped, and
hand anyone able to corrupt a response the power to do it deliberately. The
entitlement document never touches Tier D in either direction; Tier D is
never gated.

## Migration And The Latch

A device provisioned under the single-document format holds a legacy
document: provisioning-signed, no `schema` marker, and legitimately carrying
`device.rated_capacity_amps` and the other keys v2 retired.

- A consumer with **no durable record of having accepted a v2 document**
  loads a legacy document under v1's rules and derives the v2 document from
  it by the migration steps in `runtime-config/v2`, preserving `device.id`.
  The derived document is held at generation 1 and marked **legacy-derived**:
  the first v2 document supersedes it whatever generation it names, because a
  producer legitimately allocates its first generation as 1 and the derivation
  was never an allocation. Freshness applies between v2 documents only. Legacy
  mode activates no profile that requires commissioned facts, because a legacy
  device has no binding.
- A legacy document carries no `signing_key`. It is verified against the
  current provisioning anchor alone, and every failure there — another key,
  the previous provisioning key, a bad signature — is `bad_signature`.
  Migration is not a bypass, and `wrong_authority` is unreachable for a
  document that cannot name its key.
- The first accepted v2 provisioning document sets the **latch**: a durable
  record that this device has crossed over. From then on a legacy document is
  refused as `legacy_refused`, permanently. A legacy document validly carries
  a safety parameter under the authority the split removed it from, so
  replaying an old valid one is exactly the reintroduction the split exists to
  prevent.
- The latch lives in the state store beside the device identity, survives
  restart and reinstall, and has no flag, command, document, or policy that
  clears it. An installer that finds runtime state at the install root is not
  performing a fresh install and MUST NOT clear it; only a genuinely empty
  root, which licenses a host-derived identity, starts unlatched.

## Rotation

Each of the provisioning and commissioning classes carries a current and an
optional previous anchor; the commercial class carries one. Rotation moves
the current anchor and demotes the old one to previous. Under the selection
rule above, an artifact newly signed by the demoted key selects the previous
anchor, verifies, and is refused as `superseded_signer` — not dismissed at
selection as a stranger, which would report a key of our own as unknown and
lose the fact that the artifact was once legitimately signed. Nothing already
accepted is affected.

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Stage | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `anchor_collision` | configuration load | the set |
| `malformed` | parses | any artifact, under its own contract |
| `unknown_signer` | key_selection | provisioning, binding |
| `bad_signature` | signature | any artifact |
| `wrong_authority` | authority | provisioning, binding |
| `superseded_signer` | authority | provisioning, binding |
| `foreign_field`, `forbidden_content` | content | provisioning, binding |
| `stale`, `generation_reused` | freshness | provisioning |
| `inventory_ahead` | composition | binding, retained pending |
| `inventory_behind` | composition | binding, not applied |
| `inventory_mismatch` | composition | the set; actuation withdrawn |
| `legacy_accepted` | migration | a legacy document on an unlatched device |
| `legacy_refused` | migration | a legacy document after the latch |
| `entitlement_retained` | application | entitlement; the previous document stays in force |
| `accepted` | application | any artifact that passed every stage above |

## Consumer Obligations

- Configure anchors out of band only, refuse `anchor_collision` at load, and
  never read an anchor from any artifact of the set.
- Select by key material across every anchor, verify against one key, and
  decide authority only over a verified document.
- Apply each artifact whole or not at all; hold the accepted set durably;
  re-evaluate the inventory invariant after every application.
- License actuation only while the invariant holds; withdraw it, report
  `inventory_mismatch`, and keep observing and alerting when it does not.
- Refuse to start under hardened posture with declared actuating hardware and
  no current binding; never claim protection in development posture without
  one.
- Retain entitlement across failed fetches and refused documents; let expiry
  revoke.
- Latch on the first accepted v2 document, durably, with no clearing path.
- Report every verdict by name, so that an authority failure is never shown as
  a signature failure and a composition failure is never shown as either.

### Vectors

[vectors/set-transitions.json](vectors/set-transitions.json) carries
sequences: a configured anchor set, an initial durable state, and events —
artifacts arriving with a named signer and generation, and startups under a
posture — each with the verdict and the resulting set state expected after
it. Anchors are labels, signatures are `valid` or `invalid`, and no case
carries bytes: the artifacts' own contracts own byte-level verification, and
this corpus proves the composition rules over their outcomes.
`scripts/check-runtime-config-orchestration-vectors` recomputes every
expectation from the rules above and refuses the corpus on disagreement.

## Open Questions

- **Commissioning key custody.** The binding contract now fixes where the
  commissioning anchors live — two environment variables in the installer's
  service environment, alongside the provisioning anchors — but issuance,
  rotation and revocation of the key itself remain open there.
- **A host-local signer.** Every host setting is provisioning-signed because
  it was in v1's single document. A fourth class for host-local settings
  would be additive here and is not proposed.
- **Entitlement selection.** Widening DevicePolicy to carry `signing_key` and
  join anchor-wide selection is a versioning decision for that contract.
